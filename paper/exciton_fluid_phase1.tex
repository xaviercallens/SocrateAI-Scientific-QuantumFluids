\begin{abstract}\noindent
A planar universal-optimality theorem for completely monotone pair interactions (OpenAI, September 2026: AI-generated and not yet
refereed; formalised in Lean, and accepted by the Comparator tool in a run by the author's own LeanMaster project, with no human audit
of the statement) says that the triangular lattice minimises the energy per particle among all locally finite planar configurations of
centred density one. We ask, as a dependency check, what it says about the interaction between the dipolar interlayer excitons of a
2026 equilibrium exciton-condensate experiment. In Lean~4, with standard axioms only, we prove that differencing preserves complete
monotonicity, so that the direct exciton--exciton kernel built from \emph{any} admissible single-layer potential is covered; the finite
telescoping identity behind the capacitor formula for the Hartree coefficient (the factor $\pi$ of the polar integral and the limit are
prose steps); that the uniform density minimises a positive-semidefinite translation-invariant interaction; and, conditionally on the
theorem taken as a hypothesis, the periodic-extension step that turns it into a bound for $N$ particles on a torus. The consequence is a
classical lower bound on the energy per particle which, at the experimental densities, lies $8$--$22$ times below the Hartree energy: the
sandwich is wide, and the bound is structural, not predictive. We prove six statements about the four-flavour mean-field model of the
experiment and reproduce T1--T3 and T5 with CVODE flows (T2 and T3 to \FFworstRel{}). We then test the theorem numerically, in a design
whose gates were fixed before the runs, with gradient flows of $N=36$ and $64$ particles in tori commensurate and incommensurate with the
lattice, integrated by rusty-SUNDIALS CVODE, with non-completely-monotone and planted-bug controls. \NPARTABSTRACT{} We report what went
wrong: a lattice-enumeration slip shared by two implementations and caught only by a closed-form constant; a compute-budget amendment; a
registered stopping rule too short for soft modes; and a control kernel of the mean-field test whose grand-canonical functional is
unbounded below (that gate failed as registered, and the control had been changed from its committed depth before the run). Producer and
verifiers of the Lean files are instances of the same model; nothing here has been refereed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In 2026 Qi \emph{et al.}\ reported evidence for two-component Bose--Einstein condensates of dipolar interlayer excitons in a
MoSe$_2$/hBN/WSe$_2$ electron--hole bilayer with an interlayer distance $d\approx 2$~nm~\cite{Qi2026}. The excitons are injected
electrically and are in equilibrium; there are four spin--valley flavours; the evidence is the spin--valley susceptibility, read through a
four-variable mean-field model; the condensate signatures persist up to about $1.8$~K (in version~1 the
Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless temperature reaches that value near the exciton Mott density
$n\approx 0.75\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$). The authors state that counterflow transport, the hallmark of exciton superfluidity, is
experimentally inaccessible in current TMD bilayers (version~1, Introduction).

In September 2026 OpenAI released a proof, with a Lean formalisation, that the triangular lattice minimises the energy per particle among
\emph{all} locally finite planar configurations of centred density one for \emph{every} smooth completely monotone function of the squared
distance~\cite{OpenAI2026}. This is the planar case of the Cohn--Kumar universal-optimality conjecture~\cite{CohnKumar2007}, whose
dimensions $8$ and $24$ were settled in 2022~\cite{CKMRV2022}.

Dipolar excitons repel each other; the interaction is a function of the distance. The question of this note is therefore a
\emph{dependency check}: which hypotheses of the theorem does the exciton physics meet, which consequences follow, and how large are
they? Our answers are given in Sections~\ref{sec:kernels} to~\ref{sec:fourflavour}; their kernel-checked parts are listed in
Section~\ref{sec:lean}, and numerical tests with rusty-SUNDIALS, a Rust implementation of the CVODE algorithms of the
SUNDIALS suite~\cite{Hindmarsh2005}, are in Section~\ref{sec:numerics}.

\begin{keybox}[title={What this note claims and what it does not}]
\textbf{Claims.} (i) A lemma, proved in Lean, that makes the theorem applicable to the exciton--exciton direct kernel; (ii) the size of the
resulting lower bound relative to the Hartree upper bound at the experimental densities; (iii) six statements about the experiment's own
model, proved in Lean (T1--T3 and T5 also reproduced by CVODE flows); (iv) the periodic-extension step that turns the theorem into a bound for $N$ particles
on a torus, proved in Lean as a statement conditional on the theorem; (v) pre-registered numerical tests of the theorem on small tori, with
controls, and of the uniform mean-field state with a nonlocal kernel.\\
\textbf{Does not claim.} The theorem is AI-generated and unrefereed; we use it as a hypothesis and test it only on finite systems, which
can fail to falsify it but cannot prove it. The Lean files were produced and checked by instances of the same model (producer
$\neq$ verifier, not human review). Nothing here describes the experiment beyond the authors' own model, whose parameters
$g_X$ and $\Delta$ are phenomenological (and differ between version~1 and the version of record); agreement of magnitude with the data is
not independent evidence. The sandwich between the lattice and Hartree energies is wide in the experimental window (a factor $8$--$22$):
the ground-state energy lies between them and nothing here locates it. No claim is made about the solver being the best of its kind. Neither the experimental group nor the authors of the
theorem have reviewed this note. It was produced overnight by an AI system (Claude, Anthropic) working autonomously under the
author's standing instructions, with two further instances as verifiers and one as adversarial reviewer; no human reviewed it
before its deposit.
\end{keybox}

\section{The theorem and its conventions}\label{sec:theorem}

Let $g\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ be smooth with $(-1)^r g^{(r)}(t)\ge0$ for all $r\ge0$, $t>0$ (``admissible'', $g\in\Adm$). For a
locally finite $C\subset\mathbb R^2$ write $N_R=\#(C\cap B_R)$, with $B_R$ the closed disk about the origin, and
\begin{equation}
E_g(C)=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac1{N_R}\sum_{\substack{x,y\in C\cap B_R\\ x\ne y}} g(|x-y|^2),
\end{equation}
the sum running over \emph{ordered} pairs. If $N_R/(\pi R^2)\to1$ (centred density one), the theorem states
$E_g(C)\ge\sum_{a\in A\setminus 0}g(|a|^2)=E_g(A)$, with $A$ the triangular lattice of covolume one, in $[0,\infty]$.
Four features matter for every use.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{Ordered pairs.} The physical energy per particle is half of $E_g$: $\elat(\rho)=\tfrac12\sum_{a\in A_\rho\setminus0}V(|a|)$,
with $A_\rho=\rho^{-1/2}A$. A factor-two slip is the likeliest error downstream; Section~\ref{sec:numerics} plants one on purpose.
\item \emph{Centred disks and a $\liminf$.} The statement is about infinite configurations. A bound for $N$ particles in a
torus needs a periodic-extension argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:sandwich}), which brings in the interaction of a particle with its own
periodic images; that step is formalised here, conditionally on the theorem.
\item \emph{Value, not minimisers.} The minimiser is not unique in this formulation: a single vacancy changes neither the density nor the
$\liminf$. Uniqueness of the triangular lattice is not part of the theorem.
\item \emph{Class, not each potential.} The proof constructs sharp Fourier minorants for every Gaussian and integrates; a sharp auxiliary
function for each completely monotone potential is not claimed~\cite{OpenAI2026}.
\end{enumerate}
The Lean statement is \texttt{OAI.AtomicTriangular.universal\_energy\_minimum}; the Comparator tool accepted its proof with
axioms $\{\text{propext},\text{Quot.sound},\text{Classical.choice}\}$ in a 140-minute run of the author's own LeanMaster project on a second
machine~\cite{LeanMaster2026} (the external kernel nanoda was not run; the statement has not been audited by a human; a
statement-fidelity audit against Definitions 1.1--1.3 of~\cite{CKMRV2022} exists, written by the AI agents of that project and therefore
not independent). That check was not repeated in this study. Predecessors are Montgomery's theorem among lattices~\cite{Montgomery1988} and the dimension-$8$ and
$24$ results~\cite{CKMRV2022}.

\section{Kernels, a lemma, and a lower bound}\label{sec:kernels}

\paragraph{The lemma.} Two interlayer excitons, each with its electron in one layer and its hole in the other at distance $d$,
interact directly through $V_{xx}(r)=2[V(r)-V(\sqrt{r^2+d^2})]$ in terms of the single-layer electron--electron potential $V$
(the electron--hole term is the second). This treats the excitons as point dipoles (the in-plane size of an exciton is neglected) and uses the
same $V$ for the intra- and interlayer terms. In the variable $t=r^2$ it is $2[g(t)-g(t+d^2)]$.

\begin{lemma}[differencing]\label{lem:diff}
If $g\in\Adm$ and $c>0$ then $t\mapsto g(t)-g(t+c)\in\Adm$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $h_r=(-1)^rg^{(r)}\ge0$. Then $h_r'=-h_{r+1}\le0$, so each $h_r$ is non-increasing. Hence
$(-1)^r\big(g-g(\cdot+c)\big)^{(r)}(t)=h_r(t)-h_r(t+c)\ge0$ for all $r\ge0$ (for $r=0$ this is non-negativity).
\end{proof}

The statement is classical (a forward difference of a completely monotone function is completely monotone, immediately from
the Bernstein representation); the point of the Lean proof is that it needs nothing beyond the definition and is mechanical. With $g(t)=t^{-1/2}$ (the Coulomb case of the Riesz family, admissible by an explicit formula for the
derivatives) the lemma gives the bilayer dipole kernel $2[t^{-1/2}-(t+d^2)^{-1/2}]$, which decays as $d^2t^{-3/2}$ and has a finite lattice
energy. The same lemma covers every exciton--exciton kernel built from an admissible single-layer potential, in particular the
Keldysh--Rytova potential, which is a positive mixture of exponentials $e^{-ur/r_0}/\sqrt{1+u^2}$ by the integral representation
of $H_0-Y_0$~\cite{DLMF} (checked numerically at three points to better than $2\times10^{-31}$ by a scratch script kept in the
repository, not proved in Lean); each exponential $e^{-c\sqrt t}$ is completely monotone in $t=r^2$ (classical: $\sqrt t$ is a Bernstein
function; not formalised); and a Yukawa potential. The potential of a dual-gated device is a sum of image charges with alternating signs; its complete monotonicity
is \emph{not} established here: we checked the sign pattern of the derivatives to order $5$ at seven values of $t$ for gate
distances $5$, $7.5$ and $10$~nm and $d=2$~nm, which is evidence and not a proof.

\paragraph{Kernel-checked.} In Lean~4 (Section~\ref{sec:lean}): Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}; that the bilayer kernel is admissible (the
Riesz case $s=1$, $t\mapsto t^{-1/2}$, is proved from Mathlib alone in a probe written by the verifying instance and integrated
into the file; the general-$s$ statement is in the LeanMaster contribution~\cite{LeanMaster2026}); and the negative control that
$e^{-t^2}$ (the cluster-crystal kernel of the generalised exponential model) is \emph{not} admissible.

\paragraph{The Hartree coefficient.} For admissible $g$ with $g\to0$ at infinity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hartree}
\int_{\mathbb R^2}\!\big[g(|x|^2)-g(|x|^2+d^2)\big]\,d^2x=\pi\!\int_0^{d^2}\!g(t)\,dt
\end{equation}
(telescoping: polar coordinates give the factor $\pi$ and the telescoping sum the limit). For $g=2t^{-1/2}$ this is $4\pi d$, in units
$e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon=1$: the capacitor formula $\tilde U(0)=e^2d/\varepsilon_0\varepsilon$, and the authors' $g_H=8\pi d$ in
Rydberg units. What Lean proves is the finite identity $\int_0^R[g(t)-g(t+c)]\,dt=\int_0^cg-\int_R^{R+c}g$ (\texttt{telescoping\_integral})
and $\int_0^{d^2}2t^{-1/2}\,dt=4d$ (\texttt{bilayer\_hartree}); the factor $\pi$ and the limit $g\to0$ are prose steps.

\paragraph{Mean field.} A completely monotone function of $r^2$ is positive definite in every dimension~\cite{Schoenberg1938}, so its Fourier
transform is non-negative. For a positive-semidefinite translation-invariant interaction on a finite abelian group, the uniform density
minimises $\sum_{x,y}n(x)n(y)U(x-y)$ at fixed mass (Lean: \texttt{uniform\_minimises}, with positive semidefiniteness as a hypothesis).
Consequently a Gross--Pitaevskii calculation with such a kernel cannot crystallise: the uniform state is its ground state, and the
Bogoliubov frequencies are real ($\omega_k^2>0$ for $k\ne0$): no dynamical instability and no roton softening (a maxon--roton
structure of $\omega_k$ is not excluded). That is a statement about mean-field theory, not about the quantum fluid.

\begin{proposition}[sandwich]\label{prop:sandwich}
Let $N$ bosons live on a torus $\mathbb R^2/\Lambda$ with $\mathrm{covol}(\Lambda)\rho=N$, with $H=-\tfrac12\Delta+\sum_{i<j}U_\Lambda(x_i-x_j)$
and $U_\Lambda$ the periodised completely monotone kernel, and let $c_\Lambda=\tfrac12\sum_{m\in\Lambda\setminus0}U(|m|)$ be the interaction of
a particle with its own periodic images. Then the ground-state energy per particle satisfies
$\elat(\rho)-c_\Lambda\le e_0(\rho)\le\tfrac12\rho\tilde U(0)(1-1/N)\le\eH(\rho)$, $\eH=\tfrac12\rho\tilde U(0)$. For the
Hamiltonian $H+Nc_\Lambda$ (the energy of the periodic extension, which is what the theorem and \texttt{torus\_lower\_bound} bound and what
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} computes) the lower bound reads $\elat(\rho)\le e_0$. For kernels that decay faster than $r^{-2}$, $c_\Lambda\to0$ as
$N\to\infty$ at fixed $\rho$, so Table~\ref{tab:sandwich} is unaffected.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
\emph{Upper bound:} the constant orbital (a variational state, kinetic energy zero) has energy per particle
$\tfrac12\rho\tilde U(0)(1-1/N)$ for a non-negative kernel.
\emph{Lower bound:} the kinetic energy is non-negative, so $e_0$ is at least the minimum of the potential energy over $N$-point
configurations; the periodic extension of a configuration has centred density $\rho$, and the theorem bounds its energy per particle,
self-images included, by $\elat(\rho)$; the energy per particle of the extension is the physical one plus $c_\Lambda$. The
periodic-extension step (the class counts of the translates of $\Lambda$ in disks, and the comparison of the disk sums with the
per-cell sum) is formalised in Lean as \texttt{torus\_lower\_bound}, \emph{conditional on the first half of the upstream
theorem, taken as a hypothesis}; the integration of the pointwise bound over a probability density is elementary and is not
formalised. Configurations with two particles at the same point of the torus are excluded by hypothesis (a set of measure zero for
any $L^2$ state).
\end{proof}

\section{The experiment's own model}\label{sec:fourflavour}

With flavours $1,2=\mathrm{KK},\mathrm{K'K'}$ (intravalley) and $3,4=\mathrm{KK'},\mathrm{K'K}$ (intervalley), the authors' energy is
\begin{equation}
H(n)=\sum_i(E_i-\mu)n_i+\tfrac12(g_H+g_X)\Big(\sum_in_i\Big)^2-g_X(n_1n_2+n_3n_4),\qquad n_i\ge0,
\end{equation}
with $E_{1,2}=\pm(g_v-g_c)\mu_BB-\Delta$, $E_{3,4}=\mp(g_c+g_v)\mu_BB$, $g_H=8\pi d$, $g_c\approx3$, $g_v\approx6$ and the
phenomenological $g_X=1$, $\Delta=1~\mu$eV of arXiv version~1 (Rydberg and Bohr-radius units; the version of record uses
$g_X=1.5$, $\Delta=0.3~\mu$eV, see below; the paper's Eqs.~(2)--(3) are quoted as printed in version~1, the main text of the version of record being behind a
paywall). A four-variable quadratic energy is exactly what a
proof assistant handles. We proved, in Lean (Section~\ref{sec:lean}):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=\textbf{T\arabic*.}]
\item for $g_X>0$ and $E_i\neq E_j$ across the two pairs, a global minimiser on the orthant has its support inside one exchange
pair, $\{1,2\}$ or $\{3,4\}$;
\item at any stationary point supported on $\{1,2\}$: $n_2-n_1=2(g_v-g_c)\mu_BB/g_X$ and $N=2(\mu+\Delta)/(2g_H+g_X)$;
\item at any stationary point supported on $\{3,4\}$: $n_3-n_4=2(g_c+g_v)\mu_BB/g_X$ and $N=2\mu/(2g_H+g_X)$;
\item on each pair subspace $H=\Omega+\tfrac14(2g_H+g_X)\,(N-N_*)^2+\tfrac14g_X\,(\delta-\delta_*)^2$, where $N,\delta$ are the sum and
difference of the pair's occupations ($n_1\pm n_2$, resp.\ $n_3\pm n_4$) and $N_*,\delta_*$ the stationary values of T2 and T3, with
$\Omega_A=-(\mu+\Delta)^2/(2g_H+g_X)-\beta_A^2/g_X$, $\Omega_B=-\mu^2/(2g_H+g_X)-\beta_B^2/g_X$, $\beta_A=(g_v-g_c)\mu_BB$,
$\beta_B=(g_c+g_v)\mu_BB$, so that for $g_X>0$, $2g_H+g_X>0$ these are the minima of $H$ on the pair subspaces;
\item $\Omega_A=\Omega_B$ iff $(\mu_BB)^2=g_X\Delta(2\mu+\Delta)/\big(4g_cg_v(2g_H+g_X)\big)$ (the first-order transition; equality
only: that II$_A$ is the ground state below $B_c$ is not proved);
\item for $g_X<0$ and $E_i\neq E_j$ every minimiser is single-component (the Hartree--Fock ferromagnet; the negative control).
\end{enumerate}
T2 and T3 are the authors' printed formulas (a confirmation). The paper prints only qualitative forms of T1 (``no more than two
flavors condense simultaneously'') and T6 (``negative $g_X$ leads to a ferromagnetic single-component condensate''); the exchange-pair
structure, the identity of T4 and the closed form of T5 are not printed. The genericity hypothesis $E_i\neq E_j$ fails on the four lines
$\Delta=\pm2g_v\mu_BB$, $\pm2g_c\mu_BB$ of the $(B,\Delta)$ plane ($B\approx1.4$ and $2.9$~mT for the quoted values). It cannot be
dropped from T1 and T6 as stated (the Lean file gives $E=(0,10,0,10)$, with a minimiser spanning both pairs); on the four physical lines
the conclusion appears to persist numerically but is not proved.

\emph{Critical field.} With the version-1 values ($g_X=1$, $\Delta=1~\mu$eV; $a_B=1.5$~nm, $\mathrm{Ry}=67$~meV) the closed form gives
$B_c=\FFBcmT$~mT at $n_x=0.5\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$ ($50.0$~mT at $0.4\times10^{12}$; their Extended Data Fig.~7c reads
``$\sim50$~mT''). The version of record states $g_X=1.5$ and $\Delta=0.3~\mu$eV as ``the parameters used in the main text'' (Extended
Data Fig.~8: $n_x=0.4\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$, energy crossing at $\sim30$~mT); with them the closed form gives $33.5$~mT at
$0.4\times10^{12}$ and $37.5$~mT at $0.5\times10^{12}$ (script \path{exploration/exciton/python/critical_field_versions.py}; $\pm3\,\%$
with the unit constants of the paper's Methods). That the order of magnitude agrees is expected, since $g_X$ and $\Delta$ were chosen
to describe the data; the experimental field windows of their Fig.~4 are averaging windows, not boundaries. The statement $B_c\propto\sqrt n$
at fixed $\Delta$ reproduces the trend they report.

One remark on the source, for readers who check against the paper: in arXiv version~1 the Ginzburg--Landau line of the Methods carries
$-(g_v-g_c)\mu_BB(n_1-n_2)$ and states $n_1-n_2=2(g_v-g_c)\mu_BB/g_X$, the opposite sign to the paper's Eq.~(3) and to its main text
($n_2-n_1=\dots$ for II$_A$); the Lean statements follow the paper's Eqs.~(2)--(3) and its main text. This concerns version~1 only; the main text of the version of
record (paywalled) was not read, only its public abstract and Extended Data legends.

(\texttt{VERIFICATION\_*.md}). This is not human review and no mathematician has read the files; the load-bearing evidence is
kernel acceptance and the axiom lists. \texttt{TorusBound.lean} is conditional on a theorem that nobody here has checked.

\section{Numerical tests on rusty-SUNDIALS}\label{sec:numerics}

All gates were fixed before the runs they govern (\path{docs/designs/EXCITON_FLUID_PHASE1_PREREG.md}, committed with
independent reference numbers produced by a separate numpy/mpmath script before the Rust code existed); two amendments, with their
reasons and dates, are recorded in the same file and below. Results are read as registered, failures included; exploratory runs
(longer flows, a diagnostic trace) are labelled as such.

\subsection{Instrument}

rusty-SUNDIALS is the author's own Rust implementation of the CVODE algorithms
(\url{https://github.com/xaviercallens/rusty-SUNDIALS}); it is not the LLNL library of~\cite{Hindmarsh2005} and has not been reviewed
by its authors. The code of this study is a small Rust crate on tree \texttt{996aaf0706e1} (tag v11.6.0, commit \texttt{6545abf};
the branch commit \texttt{5db8041} actually used has the same tree), using the \texttt{cvode} crate with the BDF method (orders 1--5),
relative tolerance $10^{-10}$ and an analytic Jacobian; all production runs use BDF. Gate S0: the crate's own tests pass ($22$ of $22$,
including the Adams-order and tight-tolerance tests); on $y'=-y$ to $t=10$ the Adams method at $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-10}$ needs
\SzeroAdamsRhs{} right-hand-side evaluations for a maximum relative error \SzeroAdamsErr{} (an older first-order build of the shared
Python environment needed $2.2\times10^{6}$ evaluations on a similar test, CLAIM-109 of the ledger), and BDF at
$\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-8}$ reaches \SzeroBdfErr.

\subsection{Known answers, and the first failure}

The energy per particle of $N$ points in a rectangular torus, with periodic images summed and the images of a point with itself included
(as in the periodic extension the theorem is applied to), is $E=\frac1{2N}\sum_{(i,j,m)\ne(i,i,0)}g(|x_i-x_j+L\!\circ\! m|^2)$; the
lattice energy is $\elat(\rho)=\tfrac12\sum_{a\ne0}g(|a|^2)$. The gates were: (KA-1a) $\sum' r^{-3}$ over the unit-spacing triangular
lattice against $6\zeta(3/2)L(3/2,\chi_{-3})=11.0341757349\ldots$; (KA-1b) the Gaussian lattice energy against the Jacobi-theta closed form;
(KA-1c) the lattice energies of all exactly summable kernels against an independent numpy sum; (KA-2) the Hartree integral
$\int V\,d^2r=4\pi d$ by quadrature; (KA-3) the Rust energy and force against an independent numpy implementation at $35$ random
configurations and the analytic Hessian against a finite difference of the force; (KA-4) the table of $\eH/\elat$.

\begin{failbox}[title={KA-1a failed as registered, first}]
The relative errors were $1.8\times10^{-4}$, $1.0\times10^{-4}$, $5.4\times10^{-5}$ at cutoffs $R=200,400,800$, halving with $R$. The lattice
enumeration of both the numpy reference and the Rust code looped over $|m|,|k|\le R/a+3$ and missed the caps $|y|>\tfrac{\sqrt3}2R$ of
the disk. Two implementations by the same author shared the slip; only the closed-form constant exposed it. Both were corrected, the
references regenerated and the gates re-run unchanged (amendment A1). The exactly summable references changed by less than $2\times10^{-16}$,
the sandwich table by at most $3\times10^{-4}$.
\end{failbox}

After the correction the gates read: KA-1a \KAaRel{} at $R=800$ (gate $10^{-6}$); KA-1b \KAbRel{}; KA-1c \KAcRel{} (and \KAcAtwoRel{} at the
reduced cutoffs of amendment A2); KA-2 \KAtwoRel{}; KA-4 \KAfourRel{}; KA-3 energy \KAthreeE{}, force \KAthreeF{} (max-norm, relative),
Hessian against finite differences \KAthreeHess{}; a Hessian with the sign flipped (planted) is detected.

\subsection{The four-flavour flow}

The imaginary-time flow $\dot\psi_i=-\psi_i\,\partial H/\partial n_i$, $n_i=\psi_i^2$, was integrated with CVODE (BDF, $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-12}$)
from \FFstarts{} random positive starts at $B=0.01$, $0.04$ and $0.2$~T. Every converged final state has its support inside one exchange
pair (T1: no cross-pair supports among $\FFstarts{}$); the polarisations and total densities of T2, T3 are reproduced to
\FFworstRel{} (gate $10^{-9}$); the field at which the grand potentials of the converged II$_A$ and II$_B$ states are equal is
$B_c=\FFBcmT$~mT, equal to the closed form to \FFBcRel{} (gate $10^{-8}$); and starting from II$_A$ with a seed of $10^{-6}$ in flavour~3 (intervalley) the state
leaves the intravalley pair at \FFspinNum{}~T against the closed-form spinodal \FFspinCF{}~T (informational, within $5\,\%$). The large metastability window
is a property of the mean-field model, not a prediction of the experiment. The grand potential decreases along every flow.

\subsection{N-particle gradient flows}

\paragraph{Design.} For each (kernel, density, torus) batch, random starts (200 for K1, for K2 at $\rho=0.5$ on T36c and for the control N1; 60 for all other K2--K4 batches under amendment A2, decided
after K1 and K2 at $\rho=0.5$ on T36c had run, which also lowered their cutoffs so that the neglected terms are of order $2\times10^{-13}\,\elat$,
KA-1c at those cutoffs) relax by $\dot x=-\nabla(NE)$ under CVODE BDF with the analytic Hessian as Jacobian, $\tau_{\max}=2000$; on
commensurate tori, basin-hopping trials (up to 200, or 60) follow only if the lattice energy was not reached by the random starts (this
happened only for K1 at $\rho=1.5$ on T36c). Tori: T36c ($6a\times3\sqrt3a$, commensurate), T64c ($8a\times4\sqrt3a$, K1 only) and T36s (square, incommensurate,

violation, $\min E/\elat-1\ge-10^{-10}$; EX-1b attainment on commensurate tori, $\le10^{-9}$ (``not attained by this search'' is not a
failure of the inequality); EX-1c frustration on incommensurate tori; EX-2 the control reaches $E/\elat\le0.99$; L2 the energy
never increases by more than $10^{-9}$ relative; NEG planted bugs must make the gates fail.

\paragraph{Results as registered.} (i) \textbf{EX-1a passes} in all 17 completely monotone cases: the smallest
$E/\elat-1$ over the \NRunsCM{} runs of these cases is \NMinRatio{}, which is the roundoff and cutoff level of the lattice sums, not a

flows met the convergence criterion by $\tau_{\max}=2000$. An exploratory continuation of the best configuration reaches
$+1.1\times10^{-15}$ at $\tau\approx4\times10^{4}$: the registered stopping rule is too short for the soft modes of a
Gaussian kernel at that density, so ``not attained by the registered search'' is the accurate statement. (iii) \textbf{EX-1c:}
the best energy found on every incommensurate torus lies above the lattice energy, by \NFrusMin{} (K1 at $\rho=1.5$,
strongly overlapping soft cores) to \NFrusMax{} (K3 at $\rho=0.1$); the exploratory flows change these by at most $11\,\%$. (iv) \textbf{EX-2 passes:} all 800 runs
of the control end below the lattice energy, the best at $E/\elat=$ \NNoneBest{} to \NNoneWorst{}; the search did not find
the best cluster crystal at $\rho=4$ (a triangular cluster crystal gives $0.661$, the search $0.723$), so it is not exhaustive.
(v) \textbf{L2:} \NLtwoViol{} violations in \NRuns{} flows. (vi) \textbf{NEG:} a doubled lattice energy (the ordered-pair slip)
makes EX-1a fail, a halved one makes EX-1b fail, and a Hessian with a sign flipped is detected by KA-3; the gates bite.
Of the \NRuns{} runs, \NNotConv{} did not meet the convergence criterion within $\tau_{\max}$ (they count with their final energy).
On T36c, 40 to 55 percent of the random starts end within $10^{-9}$ of the lattice energy ($90/200$ for K1 at $\rho=0.5$; $84/200$ and
$33/60$ for K2 at $\rho=0.5$ and $1.5$; $27/60$ for K3 and for K4 at $\rho=0.5$), none for K1 at $\rho=1.5$ within the registered flow length,
and $5/60$ and $1/60$ for K3 and K4 at the low density $\rho=0.1$ where the kernels are long-ranged and flat; $46/200$ on T64c.

\paragraph{What this does and does not show.} The energies are upper bounds on the minimum energy of $N$ points on the torus;
that they equal the lattice energy to roundoff on commensurate tori, and never go below it, is the behaviour the theorem
predicts and is consistent with a correct implementation (two implementations by the same author and a closed-form constant agree,
KA gates); on incommensurate tori nothing is predicted beyond positivity. It does not prove the theorem: a local search from
random starts at $N\le64$ can only fail to find a counterexample.

\subsection{Nonlocal-kernel mean field (Phase 2)}

A second test (\path{docs/designs/EXCITON_FLUID_PHASE2_PREREG.md}, committed as \texttt{363475b} with independent reference numbers
before any run, with a control kernel of depth $40$ that the run then did not use: see the end of this subsection) uses the mean-field
functional $\Omega[\psi]=\int\tfrac12|\nabla\psi|^2+\tfrac12 n\,(U\!\ast n)-\mu n$ on a
$16\times16$ periodic grid (256 unknowns, within CVODE's dense Newton solver), with the bilayer kernel $\tilde U_B(k)=4\pi(1-e^{-kd})/k$
(kernel B, positive; units $\hbar=m=1$, $d=1$, $L=16$, $\mu=0.25$) and a control kernel C obtained by subtracting a Gaussian band of depth
$28$ and width $0.2$ at $k_0=2\pi\cdot3/L$ (not completely monotone). Results as registered:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{P2-a, pass.} From 50 random starts the imaginary-time flow of kernel B reaches the uniform state in all runs (by $\tau=100$,
about 1100 BDF steps); worst $\max|n-n_0|/n_0=$ \PtwoAdev{} and worst relative grand-potential error \PtwoAom{}.
\item \textbf{P2-b, pass.} The solver's own linearisation (central differences of the real-time right-hand side about the uniform state)
reproduces the Bogoliubov spectrum $\omega_k^2=\varepsilon_k(\varepsilon_k+2n_0\tilde U(k))$ on all 255 nonzero wavenumbers to
\PtwoBomTwo{} (relative error of $\omega^2$; \PtwoBom{} in $\omega$) with real parts of the eigenvalues below \PtwoBre.
\item \textbf{P2-c(i), pass.} For kernel C the same linearisation reproduces $\omega^2$ to \PtwoCom{} and finds the same \PtwoCn{} unstable modes as the formula.
\item \textbf{P2-d, pass.} A planted bug ($\tilde U(0)$ doubled) is caught on all 5 starts (density deviation $0.5$).
\item \textbf{P2-c(ii), \emph{failed as registered}.} The flow of kernel C from uniform plus noise ended with a CVODE convergence failure in
the first chunk, so the registered evidence (a modulation $\ge0.1$ and a grand potential below the uniform one by $10^{-3}$) was
not produced. A re-run with relaxed tolerances failed identically; a diagnostic trace in short chunks (exploratory) shows
the grand potential decreasing monotonically while the modulation grows exponentially (rate $\approx0.12$), crossing $0.1$ at
$\tau\approx38$, before the integration runs away at $\tau\approx58$. The reason is analytic, and it is a flaw of the control's design: at
fixed $\mu$ the functional of kernel C is \emph{unbounded below} (a stripe of Gaussian profile has interaction energy
$\tfrac12\bar n^2F(w)$ with $\min_wF=-19$; script \path{exploration/exciton/python/phase2_boundedness.py}), so no minimiser exists (the
uniform state is an unstable stationary point); in this family a bounded, linearly unstable control does not exist at fixed $\mu$. The
failure therefore says nothing against the solver or the Lean statement, but the gate stays failed.
\item \textbf{History of the control.} The registration committed as \texttt{363475b} had depth $40$ (reference $\min\omega^2=-0.42$). Within a
minute, before any run, a boundedness check (a cosine profile only, which was itself incomplete, see above) led to depth $28$: the
reference numbers were regenerated and amendment A1 was written into the registration file, but A1 carries no date and was
committed only with the results (\texttt{3203d78}). The control was therefore changed after registration and before the run, and the
Phase~2 registration is weaker than that of Phase~1 in this respect.
\end{itemize}
This phase supports the consistency of \texttt{uniform\_minimises} and of the Bogoliubov spectrum with the solver on the bilayer
kernel and says nothing about excitons beyond that bare kernel (no screening, exchange, temperature or correlations).

\section{Limitations and what we do not know}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The theorem.} It is AI-generated, unrefereed, and was accepted by the Comparator tool in a run of the author's own project on
another machine; that check was not repeated here, and the adequacy of the statement has been audited only by AI agents (the same
project's, not independent). The same upstream release (7 October 2026) withdrew three other manuscripts of that body of
AI-generated work (on Hodge classes and K3 surfaces) after a sign error was found; neither triangular manuscript was withdrawn.
That calibrates the word ``unrefereed''. Everything marked ``conditional'' above is conditional on
the theorem. A finite-$N$ numerical test can fail to falsify it; it cannot prove it.
\item \textbf{Tightness.} In the experimental window the sandwich between the lattice and the Hartree energies is wide (Table~1: a
factor $8$--$22$); the ground-state energy lies between them and nothing here locates it. The lower bound is a structural statement and
a solver test, not a prediction.
\item \textbf{Kernels.} Complete monotonicity of the dual-gated device kernel is numerical evidence, not a proof; the Keldysh
representation was checked numerically, not in Lean; positive definiteness of completely monotone kernels is classical
(Schoenberg) and a hypothesis in Lean.
\item \textbf{The mean-field test} (Phase 2) is a test of the solver and of \texttt{uniform\_minimises} on a bare bilayer kernel,
not of exciton physics (no screening, exchange, temperature, correlations); its control failed as registered, for a reason that
is a flaw of the control's design, and the control had been changed from its committed depth before the run.
\item \textbf{N-particle tests} use $N=36$ and $64$, three tori, a local search from random starts, and (for K2--K4) the reduced
budget of amendment A2; K5 and K6 were not run. The registered stopping rule is too short for the soft modes of the Gaussian
kernel at $\rho=1.5$ (EX-1b failed as registered there; exploratory longer flows attain the lattice energy to $10^{-15}$).
\item \textbf{The experiment.} Of Qi \emph{et al.} only the arXiv version~1 text was read, plus the public abstract and Extended Data legends
of the version of record (whose parameters differ); $g_X$ and $\Delta$ are phenomenological; no experimental data were used; the
four-flavour theorems are statements about the authors' own model (the authors' public code, which adds a phase term to the energy, was
not used).
\item \textbf{Lean.} Producer and verifiers are instances of one model; no human has read the files; two statements of
\texttt{FourFlavour.lean} (T2, T3) are conditional on stationarity; the library's Comparator runs predate the toolchain alignment.
\item \textbf{The solver.} CVODE dense, $\le128$ unknowns in the N-particle runs and $256$ in the mean-field runs; no claim is made
that it is the best of its kind, and no GPU or other code was measured.
\item \textbf{Not contacted.} Neither the experimental group nor the authors of the theorem have seen this note.
\end{itemize}
