\emph{Added in version~4 (\texttt{exploration/exciton/three\_d/H1d/}, pre-registration and frozen code \texttt{4e596f0}, results
\texttt{dd67a12}, after review \texttt{c977fa7}).} Version~3's certified values are lower bounds on the two-point bound; they
cannot show that the two-point bound stays below the lattice energy, which needs an \emph{upper} bound on the LP optimum.

\textbf{Method.} We adapt the discrete-reduction dual of Li~\cite{Li22}, who used it for the Cohn--Elkies sphere-packing LP, to
the energy LP of~\cite[Prop.~2.2]{CdCI16} at density~1, $\mathrm{LP}(\alpha)=\sup_h \hat h(0)-h(0)$. Fix rational $s>1$ and an
integer $m\ge2$, fold the samples of an admissible $h$ on $s^{-1}\mathbb Z^d$ modulo $m$, $G(x)=\sum_{n\equiv x}h(n/s)$, and put
$\Gamma(x)=\sum_{n\equiv x,\,n\ne0}e^{-\alpha|n|^2/s^2}$. Poisson summation gives $\hat G\ge0$ and $\hat h(0)\le s^{-d}\sum_xG(x)$;
$h\le g$ off $0$ gives $G\le\Gamma$ off $0$ and $-h(0)\le-G(0)+\Gamma(0)$. For any $\lambda\ge0$ on $\mathbb Z_m^d$ with
$\sum\lambda=1-s^{-d}$ and $\mu(x)=s^{-d}+\sum_y\lambda(y)\cos(2\pi\langle x,y\rangle/m)\ge0$ for $x\ne0$, weak duality yields
\[
\mathrm{LP}(\alpha)\;\le\;\mathrm{UB}=\Gamma(0)+\sum_{x\ne0}\mu(x)\Gamma(x).
\]
$\lambda$ comes from a floating-point LP over signed-permutation orbits (HiGHS, interior point, with $\mu\ge10^{-8}$); it is then
made exactly rational and renormalised, and $\mu$ (at every orbit) and UB are evaluated in arb with rigorous theta-sum tails. The
registered class of $h$ assumed decay $O((1+|x|)^{-d-\delta})$ of $h$ and $\hat h$; a lemma added after registration (a pre-run
reviewer's argument, checked by us; Gaussian damping $h\,e^{-\pi\varepsilon|x|^2}$ and a weak form of Poisson summation) removes
the decay and radial assumptions, so UB bounds the LP bound for every $h$ admitted by~\cite[Prop.~2.2]{CdCI16}. Cohn and
Kumar's original, wider formulation is not claimed. A literature search (CdCI, Li, two alphaXiv searches, one web search) found
dual bounds only for sphere packing; we did not find a published rigorous upper bound on the Gaussian-energy LP bound in
$d=3$, but the search was limited.

\textbf{Gates and runs.} V1 ($d=1$, $\alpha=\pi$, where the LP bound is sharp): certified $\mathrm{UB}$ within $7.3\times10^{-8}$
(relative) of $E_{\mathbb Z}$, inside the registered $10^{-6}$: pass. V2 ($d=2$, $\alpha=\pi$): consistent with the certified
lower bound, $0.71\%$ above $E_{A_2}$ (not gated). In $d=3$, six grids $(s,m)$ per $\alpha$ were registered; 29 of the 31 runs
certified, and two ($\alpha=\pi$ at $(8,48)$, $\alpha=8$ at $(6,48)$) ended with a solver status ``unknown'' and gave no bound;
they were not rerun. Each grid's UB is valid on its own, so reporting the smallest is not a selection effect.

\textbf{Reading.} At $\alpha=2,4,8$ the two-point bound in the form of~\cite[Prop.~2.2]{CdCI16} lies strictly below
$\min(E_{\rm FCC},E_{\rm BCC})$, so it cannot prove FCC or BCC optimal there; this certifies the gaps that version~2 reported
numerically. At $\alpha=\pi$, FCC and BCC are not the putative ground state (a coexistence state is lower~\cite{CKS09}); there the
relevant statement is that UB lies $0.00724$ below the record $0.23153532$ quoted in~\cite{CdCI16}, so the two-point bound cannot
prove that state optimal either. The interval $[B,\mathrm{UB}]$ at $\alpha=\pi$ contains the numerically optimised value
$0.22321782$ of~\cite{CdCI16}; the gap there was visible numerically before, and the certificate is what is new. The prediction
(V1, V2 pass; all four $d=3$ cases below $\min(E_{\rm FCC},E_{\rm BCC})$, $\alpha=2$ with the smallest margin; below the record at
$\alpha=\pi$) held in full. A pre-run review of the mathematics and code found no blocking item; it found an evaluator flaw
(a void case would not have gated the cases already evaluated), which was fixed before evaluation, when 21 of the 31 records
existed (\texttt{H1d/DEVIATIONS.md}); the fix only tightens the criterion. A results review re-verified all 29 certificates
with independent code in 256-bit arithmetic. Not shown: anything about three-point or semidefinite bounds, the true minimal
energy, other $\alpha$, or where in $[B,\mathrm{UB}]$ the LP bound lies.
