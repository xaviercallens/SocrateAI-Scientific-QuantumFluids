\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We report a small, fully pre-registered validation kit for collisionless damping, spanning a plasma and
a Fermi liquid, and we report it including the predictions that failed. (i)~The least-damped root of the
linearised Vlasov--Poisson dispersion relation for a Maxwellian is enclosed with a computer-assisted
certificate in ball arithmetic: at $k\lambda_D=0.5$,
$\omega = 1.4156618886045364\ldots - 0.1533594669096048\ldots\,i$, certified to $10^{-40}$; we found no
prior certified enclosure. (ii)~A semi-Lagrangian solver reproduces the certified rate to $0.14\%$ and
distinguishes neighbouring wavenumbers, reproduces the free-streaming recurrence time exactly, and
reproduces a closed-form ballistic plasma echo to $2\times10^{-9}$. (iii)~Two predictions that we
transferred from free streaming to the self-consistent plasma---the recurrence time and the echo
time---\emph{failed} their pre-registered tolerances, both by about $9\%$, and a resolution study shows
the shift is physical. (iv)~In Lean~4 we prove that undamped zero sound in a Fermi liquid exists if and
only if the Landau parameter is positive, in three dimensions and in two, the latter being the geometry
of neutron-scattering experiments on $^3$He films; this is textbook physics, newly machine-checked.
(v)~We use cubical persistent homology strictly as an instrument with known-answer controls. A
duality control holds exactly on simulation data. The two instruments built on it \emph{both failed}
their pre-registered criteria---a density-only vortex detector (precision $0.87$, recall $0.43$ against
phase-winding ground truth) and a phase-space hole counter---and for one identifiable reason: $H_0$
persistence is depth relative to a connecting saddle, and in both systems the objects of interest share
a valley. Of twenty-five pre-registered criteria and predictions, fourteen were met and eleven were not; three
of the eleven were errors in our own criteria or measurement design, and are reported as such.

Nothing here is new physics. Nonlinear Landau damping has just been formalized in Lean by
Bedrossian~\cite{Bedrossian2026}; our Lean results are elementary by comparison and one of them is
subsumed by that work. The contribution is the discipline: every number below was preceded by a
committed statement of what it should be, and the record of which of those statements were wrong.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Landau damping of a plasma wave and Landau damping of zero sound in liquid $^3$He are one
phenomenon: a collective mode of a collisionless kinetic equation loses energy to particles moving at
its phase velocity. The first is the standard acceptance test of every Vlasov code; the second decides
whether a collective mode of a Fermi liquid is sharp or broad in a neutron-scattering
spectrum~\cite{Godfrin2012}. This paper builds a small toolkit across that bridge, with one rule: a
quantity is computed only after the value it should take, and the tolerance, have been written down and
committed. The pre-registration, its three dated amendments, and every script are in the repository.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{What is and is not claimed.} \emph{New, and small:} a certified enclosure of the Landau root
(\S\ref{sec:k1}); a machine-checked zero-sound criterion in 2D and 3D (\S\ref{sec:lean}); a measured, and
poor, error rate for prominence-based vortex detection from density alone, with the mechanism of its
failure (\S\ref{sec:tda}). \emph{Not new:}
the benchmark itself, the plasma echo~\cite{Gould1967}, phase mixing, and---emphatically---anything
resembling a theorem about nonlinear Landau damping, which is
Mouhot--Villani's~\cite{MouhotVillani2011} and, in Lean, Bedrossian's~\cite{Bedrossian2026}.
\end{keybox}

We began this work believing that a Lean formalization of nonlinear Landau damping was out of reach, and
wrote as much in the project's planning document. The literature check, run before any code, found that
it had been posted six days earlier. That planning document now carries an erratum. The episode is the
reason \S\ref{sec:lean} is modest, and an instance of this project's recurring lesson: search first.

\section{A certified Landau root}
\label{sec:k1}

In units $\omega_p=v_{\rm th}=\lambda_D=1$, for a Maxwellian background,
\begin{equation}
D(\omega,k) = 1+\frac{1}{k^2}\bigl[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)\bigr],\qquad \zeta=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt2\,k},\qquad
Z(\zeta) = i\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-\zeta^2}\operatorname{erfc}(-i\zeta).
\end{equation}
Written this way $Z$ is entire, so the formula is already Landau's analytic continuation to
$\operatorname{Im}\omega<0$ with no case distinction. We evaluate $D$ and $D'$ (using
$Z'=-2(1+\zeta Z)$) in Arb ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb} and apply the interval Newton operator
$N(B)=m-D(m)/D'(B)$ to a square ball $B$ with midpoint $m$: if $N(B)\subset\operatorname{int}B$, then $D$
has exactly one zero in $B$. All rounding error is inside the balls.

0.4 & $1.285056969653746093937441697179005657870$ & $-0.06612795869074925181490085255855642353114$\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Certified least-damped roots; every digit shown is inside a ball of radius below $3\times10^{-40}$
proved to contain exactly one zero.}
\label{tab:roots}
\end{table}

The standard numerical reference is Canosa~\cite{Canosa1973}, $\gamma=-0.15336$ at $k=0.5$, with which
Table~\ref{tab:roots} agrees in every digit he gives. A search for a prior computer-assisted enclosure of
this root found none; that is an absence of hits, not a proof of absence. The same code certifies the
purely growing root of the two-stream background used in \S\ref{sec:tda}.

\paragraph{Controls.} A ball of radius $10^{-3}$ about $1.4156-0.1433i$---the right frequency with the
damping rate off by $0.01$---is \emph{not} certified; the same ball about the true root is.

\paragraph{What is certified.} A zero of the formula above. That the formula describes the physics is
not something ball arithmetic can know; it is tested against an independent solver in
\S\ref{sec:k2}.

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{A failed criterion.} Our pre-registered pass condition was that the certified midpoint ``rounds
to'' the literature's $1.4156$ and $-0.1533$. It rounds to $1.4157$ and $-0.1534$: the literature values
are truncations. By the letter the criterion failed, through our wording. We record it rather than
reword it.
\end{failbox}

\section{A solver validated against the certificate}
\label{sec:k2}

The solver is deliberately ordinary: Strang splitting, exact Fourier shift in $x$, cubic-spline
semi-Lagrangian advection in $v$, FFT Poisson solve. Parameters were fixed in advance
($N_x=64$, $N_v=256$, $v_{\max}=6$, $\Delta t=0.1$, perturbation $0.01\cos kx$, rate from a line through
the maxima of $\ln|E_1|$ in $5\le t\le25$).

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llllll@{}}
\toprule
run & $k$ & measured $\gamma$ & error & measured $\omega$ & error \\
\midrule
A & 0.5 & $-0.15358$ & $0.14\%$ & $1.41521$ & $0.03\%$ \\
B & 0.4 & $-0.06637$ & $0.36\%$ & $1.28485$ & $0.02\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Against the certified roots; tolerances were $2\%$ and $1\%$. Run~B misses the $k=0.5$ root by
$57\%$: the comparison can tell wavenumbers apart, which is what makes Run~A informative. Mass drifts by $7\times10^{-10}$ over $t=50$, which \emph{fails} our pre-registered $10^{-10}$; we believe, but have
not verified, that this is the zero-fill beyond $\pm v_{\max}$ in the spline step. Energy drift, for which we set no
threshold, is below $6\times10^{-7}$.}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Recurrence.} The velocity quadrature aliases the free-streaming phase $e^{-ikvt}$ at
$T_R=2\pi/(k\Delta v)$. We pre-registered that at $N_v=32$ ($T_R=33.51$) the largest maximum of $|E_1|$
in $25<t<45$ would fall within $5\%$ of $T_R$.

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Failed: $36.5$, an error of $8.9\%$.} A diagnostic written \emph{after} the failure, and
labelled as such in the repository, shows why. With the field switched off, the density mode recurs at
$t=33.50$ with amplitude ratio $1.000$: the formula is exact for what it describes. With the field on,
the recurring perturbation drives a Langmuir oscillation and the field's largest maximum lags by $3.0$,
about two thirds of an oscillation period. We had applied a free-streaming formula to a self-consistent
quantity.
\end{failbox}

\section{The plasma echo}
\label{sec:k3}

A perturbation in mode $k_1$ at $t=0$ phase-mixes away; a second in mode $k_2$ at $t=\tau$ does the
same; at second order their product contains a term whose velocity phase unwinds at
\begin{equation}
t_e=\tau\,\frac{k_2}{k_2-k_1},
\end{equation}
producing a macroscopic response in mode $k_2-k_1$ from two disturbances that have both
disappeared~\cite{Gould1967}. Controlling the accumulation of such echoes is the central difficulty of
the nonlinear theory~\cite{MouhotVillani2011}. For free streaming with multiplicative pulses of amplitude
$\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ the response is closed-form,
\begin{equation}
\rho_{k_2-k_1}(t) = \tfrac12\alpha_1\alpha_2\,
  \exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac12[(k_2-k_1)t-k_2\tau]^2\bigr),
\label{eq:echo}
\end{equation}
the Gaussian being the transform of the Maxwellian, which we also prove in Lean
(\lean{maxwellian\_mode}, \S\ref{sec:lean}). With $k_1=0.5$, $k_2=1.5$, $\tau=10$, so $t_e=15$:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Field off.} The solver matches \eqref{eq:echo}, prefactor included, to
  $2\times10^{-9}$ of the peak (tolerance $10^{-3}$), peaking at $t=15.00$. Without the second pulse the
  echo mode stays at $10^{-12}$ of the peak.
\item \textbf{Field on.} We predicted a peak within $5\%$ of $15.0$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Failed: $16.40$, an error of $9.3\%$}, with amplitude $2.08$ times the ballistic value. Doubling
the velocity resolution and halving the time step gives $16.400$ again and the same amplitude to
$0.04\%$, so the shift is not numerical: the self-consistent field delays and amplifies the echo. The
field-on negative control also failed its $1\%$ threshold ($1.5\%$): a single pulse generates its own
second harmonic at order $\alpha_1^2$, which we had not allowed for. Both are known physics---the
echo's shape depends on the dielectric response---that we did not apply when writing the prediction.
\end{failbox}

\paragraph{The pattern.} Every exact known answer was reproduced to near rounding error. Both
predictions that failed were free-streaming formulas we had transferred to the interacting system, and
both were off by about $9\%$ at these parameters. For a validation suite the practical conclusion is to
test recurrence and echo timing \emph{with the field off}, where they are exact, and to treat the
field-on values as measurements.

\section{Machine-checked statements}
\label{sec:lean}

All in Lean~4.34.0-rc2 with Mathlib, standard axioms only, each with a perturbed statement that is
required to fail, inside a library of 145 theorems described elsewhere~\cite{CallensLean}.

\paragraph{Zero sound.} With a single Landau parameter $F=F_0^s$, a collective mode of phase velocity
$s\,v_F$ satisfies $1+F\,\Omega(s)=0$; it is undamped iff $s>1$, outside the particle--hole continuum,
and Landau-damped otherwise~\cite{BaymPethick}. In three dimensions
$\Omega(s)=1-\tfrac s2\ln\frac{s+1}{s-1}$; in two, $\Omega(s)=1-s/\sqrt{s^2-1}$.
\begin{verbatim}
theorem zero_sound_iff (F : R) :
    (exists s, 1 < s /\ s / 2 * log ((s + 1) / (s - 1)) - 1 = 1 / F) <-> 0 < F
theorem zero_sound_2d_iff (F : R) :
    (exists s, 1 < s /\ 1 + F * (1 - s / sqrt (s ^ 2 - 1)) = 0) <-> 0 < F
\end{verbatim}
(ASCII rendering.) The 3D proof brackets $g(s)=\tfrac s2\ln\frac{s+1}{s-1}-1$ between
$\tfrac12\ln\frac{2}{s-1}-1$ and $1/(s-1)$, using Mathlib's $\frac{2x}{x+2}<\ln(1+x)$ for positivity,
and closes with the intermediate value theorem. In 2D the root is explicit,
$s=(1+F)/\sqrt{1+2F}$. Replacing $0<F$ by $0\le F$ fails to compile in both. The 2D case is included
because the neutron-scattering observation of a collective mode in a Fermi liquid by Godfrin et
al.~\cite{Godfrin2012} is on a monolayer, where the logarithmic formula does not apply. Uniqueness of the
3D root, $F_1^s$, finite temperature and the damped branch are not treated.

\paragraph{Phase mixing.} For any $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ and $k\neq0$,
$\int g(v)e^{-ikvt}\,dv\to0$; $e^{ik(x-vt)}g(v)$ solves the transport equation; and for the unit
Maxwellian the mode is exactly $e^{-k^2t^2/2}$. The first is a reparametrisation of Mathlib's
Riemann--Lebesgue lemma and is subsumed by~\cite{Bedrossian2026}; it is here because
\eqref{eq:echo} is a known answer, and a known answer should be a checked one.

\section{Persistent homology as an instrument}
\label{sec:tda}

This project has previously made three topological proposals about quantum fluids; one was refuted by
simulation, one withdrawn, and one stopped at the literature check as a renaming of topographic
prominence~\cite{CallensLean}. We therefore framed this section as instruments with known-answer
controls, and state at the outset what the central quantity is: the $H_0$ sublevel bar of a local
minimum is (value at the minimum, value at the saddle where its basin joins a deeper one). Its length is
prominence, upside down. Nothing below is new mathematics. All computations use GUDHI~\cite{GUDHI}
cubical complexes.

\subsection{A duality control, and why it matters}

For a function on a closed surface, finite $H_0$ sublevel pairs $(b,d)$ of $\rho$ correspond to finite
$H_1$ sublevel pairs $(-d,-b)$ of $-\rho$~\cite{CSEH2009}. On pixel data this holds exactly only if the
two sides use \emph{dual} cubical constructions---pixels as top-dimensional cells on one side, as
vertices on the other---and with periodic identification these are dual filtrations of the
torus~\cite{Bleile2022}. We pre-registered both the agreement and its converse: with the \emph{same}
construction on both sides the diagrams must differ, since the two sides then use different pixel
connectivities.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
field & dual constructions & same construction \\
\midrule
smooth random, $128^2$ & $81$ vs $81$ pairs, max difference $0$ & $81$ vs $116$ pairs \\
$|\psi|^2$, Gross--Pitaevskii, $1024^2$ & $985$ vs $985$ pairs, max difference $0$ & $985$ vs $1772$ pairs \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The duality control. Exact agreement, and exact disagreement where predicted.}
\end{table}

A first version of our check that GUDHI's cell indices map to the right pixels compared a value with
itself through the same indexing and could not fail; it was replaced by the test that every reported
birth pixel is a local minimum ($986/986$). The control is not decoration: it says that the $H_1$
superlevel diagram carries the same numbers as the $H_0$ sublevel one, so when the instruments below
failed, changing homological degree was already known not to be a remedy.

\subsection{Vortices from density alone: refuted in compressible turbulence}

Experiments on condensates image $|\psi|^2$, not the phase, so the winding number that defines a vortex
is not available; vortices are counted by blob detection or learned detectors~\cite{Metz2021}, and
persistent homology has been applied to condensate densities for other
purposes~\cite{Spitz2021,Leykam2022}. We asked what a threshold-on-prominence detector achieves against
winding-number ground truth, on our own two-dimensional Gross--Pitaevskii decaying turbulence
($\xi/\Delta x=8$, frames at $t=5,10,20$, about $830$ vortices). Fixed in advance: a detection is a minimum
with value below $0.1\,n_0$ and depth above $0.5\,n_0$, matched one-to-one within $\xi$; ``usable'' meant
precision $\ge0.95$ and recall $\ge0.90$.

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Failed: precision $0.871$, recall $0.433$.} We had predicted that recall would fail only for
close pairs, and would be at least $0.97$ for vortices with no neighbour within $2\xi$. It is $0.51$.
Isolation in position does not imply isolation in the sublevel-set topology.
\end{failbox}

A diagnostic written afterwards locates the cause. At $t=10$, \emph{all} $289$ vortices have a density
minimum below $0.1\,n_0$ within $\xi$. Their depths are below $0.1$ for $62$, $0.1$--$0.3$ for $52$,
$0.3$--$0.5$ for $46$, and above $0.5$ for only $129$. The state is strongly compressible---density
standard deviation $0.45\,n_0$, $15.5\%$ of the area below $0.5\,n_0$---so cores are joined by low-density
channels and lose their prominence; and $142$ of the $462$ near-zero minima belong to no vortex. No pair
of thresholds on our sensitivity grid exceeds recall $0.58$. The negative control passed (a vortex-free
frame with density between $0.18$ and $1.60\,n_0$: no detections among $506$ minima). Whether the detector
works in a quiet condensate, the experimentally relevant case, we did not test and do not claim.

\subsection{Phase-space holes: refuted}

For the two-stream instability ($f_0=\tfrac12[M(v-2.4)+M(v+2.4)]$, $k=0.2$) the purely growing root is
certified as in \S\ref{sec:k1}: $\gamma=0.22584425503471\ldots$

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Failed: measured growth rate $0.18656$, an error of $17\%$} (tolerance $5\%$). Either the
certificate or the measurement was wrong, so we tested both. Direct quadrature of $D(i\gamma)$, valid
without continuation for $\operatorname{Im}\omega>0$, gives $8\times10^{-15}$ at the certified root and
$-0.54$ at the measured value. The pre-registered seed of $10^{-3}$ left only two decades below
saturation, and the local growth rate slides from $0.33$ to $0.21$ without a plateau: there was no
linear phase to fit. With a seed of $10^{-8}$ the rate plateaus at $0.22591$, $0.22585$, and a fit gives
$0.22594$, within $0.04\%$ of the certificate. The solver was right and our measurement design was not.
\end{failbox}

We then counted holes as $H_0$ classes born at $|v|<2.4$ with depth above $0.1\max f$ (the treatment of
the essential class was fixed by a dated amendment before any two-stream data existed). Predicted: one
hole at $t=60$ with one wavelength in the box; two at saturation with two.

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Both failed.} With one wavelength the count is $1$ until saturation, then $8$, $29$, $14$, $52$
at $t=50,\ldots,80$: the trapped region filaments, the filaments are deep, and a depth threshold does not
tell a filament from a hole. With two wavelengths the two physically equivalent holes score depths
$0.84$ and $0.17$ at saturation: the beams leave a trough at $v\approx0$ where $f_0$ is $11\%$ of its
maximum, the holes are joined through it, and by the elder rule only one keeps a long bar. The negative
control (a stable Maxwellian) correctly gave zero.
\end{failbox}

\paragraph{One mechanism.} Both instruments failed the same way. Persistence measures depth relative to
the \emph{connecting saddle}; vortex cores in compressible turbulence and phase-space holes between two
beams both sit in a shared valley, where that saddle is low. By the duality control the other
homological degree reports the same numbers. For these two problems prominence is the wrong
discriminator, and it is the geometry of the field, not a choice of threshold, that makes it so.


\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The scorecard.} Twenty-five criteria and predictions were committed before the corresponding
code existed. Fourteen were met: the certificate and its control; both Landau runs, with the
discrimination test; the ballistic echo and its field-off control; the three Lean statements; both
halves of the duality control; one sub-prediction about close vortex pairs; and both topological
negative controls. Eleven were not. Three were our own errors of design: a rounding criterion written
against truncated literature values, a seed too large to leave a linear phase, and a negative-control
threshold that ignored self-generated harmonics. One, mass conservation at $7\times10^{-10}$ against a
threshold of $10^{-10}$, we cannot yet account for. Two were free-streaming formulas misapplied to the
interacting plasma. Five were wrong predictions about what persistence would detect.

\paragraph{What we take from it.} First, the exact known answers---certified root, ballistic echo,
free-streaming recurrence---were all reproduced at or near rounding error, and are the tests we
recommend; the self-consistent echo and recurrence times should be treated as measurements, not
checks. Second, a certificate earns its keep when a measurement disagrees with it: the $17\%$
two-stream discrepancy was resolved in minutes because one side of the comparison could not be wrong in
the way the other could, and an independent quadrature confirmed which. Third, persistent homology of a
scalar field is prominence, and prominence fails wherever the features of interest share a valley;
that is a statement about two specific configurations, not about topological data analysis.

\paragraph{What this is for.} The kit is offered as validation infrastructure for anyone writing a
Vlasov code or analysing collective modes in a Fermi liquid. It makes no claim on the mathematics of
Landau damping, whose nonlinear theory~\cite{MouhotVillani2011} now has a formal
proof~\cite{Bedrossian2026}; the dispersion relation and Penrose criterion, which that formalization
explicitly leaves aside, are where certified numerics of the present kind might eventually meet it.

\paragraph{Process note.} All predictions, analysis, code and text were produced with an AI assistant
under the author's direction; the pre-registration discipline exists precisely because such a process
generates plausible claims faster than they can be checked by reading.
