\emph{Added in version~3 (\texttt{exploration/exciton/three\_d/H1pp/}, pre-registration and frozen code \texttt{b1d2de5}).}
Version~2 left rigorous certification open. Its obstacle was arithmetic: with the constraint rewritten as $f/g\le1$, the
$O(1)$ terms of $f$ must cancel to the size of $g$ ($10^{-14}$ at the first contacts), about 23 significant digits, which
double precision does not have. H1$''$ is the different construction that the version~2 development notes called for.

\textbf{Method.} Same ansatz as H1 ($\hat f=e^{-t/2}Q(t)$, $f=\beta^{d/2}e^{-u/2}R(u)$, Laguerre coefficients $c_k$, odd effective
degree $39$ at $K=40$). (i)~The LP is solved by a primal--dual interior-point method in 256-bit arithmetic (python-flint arb
midpoints), with rows $f/g\le1$ up to $g=10^{-12}$, $R\le-10^{-12}$ beyond, $Q\ge0$ on $t\in[0,\infty)$ (log grid far out).
(ii)~Cutting planes come from \emph{exact} objects: after each round the coefficients are made dyadic-rational and the real roots
of $R'-\kappa R$ (which contain every local maximum of $f/g$), of $R$ beyond the last contact and of $Q$ are isolated
rigorously; violated regions receive rows. (iii)~Every round's coefficient vector is \emph{certified} in interval arithmetic:
$R<0$ beyond its largest real root; $f/g\le1+\tau_0$ on the rest by bisection with Taylor-shift enclosures; $\hat f+\delta
e^{-\pi|y|^2/(\lambda\beta)}\ge0$ (a Gaussian lift, $\lambda\ge1/b$, which adds at most $\delta(\lambda\beta)^{d/2}$ to $f/g$), so
that $f_{\rm tot}\le(1+\tau)g$ and the Cohn--Kumar proposition in the form of~\cite[Prop.~2.2]{CdCI16} gives the certified
bound $B=[\hat f(0)+\delta-f(0)-\delta(\lambda\beta)^{d/2}]/(1+\tau)$, whose lower interval end is reported. The best
certified $B$ over at most 25 rounds is kept (any certified vector is a valid bound, so the choice cannot invalidate it).
Development was at non-gate parameters only and found four defects of our own before registration (a real-root filter that
saw no roots, a $6\times10^{-7}$ spike of $f/g$ at a contact missed by a 20\,000-point grid, row pruning that made violations
recur, too narrow a lift); they are listed in \texttt{H1pp/DEV\_NOTES.md}.

\textbf{H1$''$ results ($K=40$, gate on V3).} V1 ($d=1$, $\alpha=1$ and $\pi$) certified $B$ within $6.7\times10^{-13}$
and $2.3\times10^{-11}$ (relative) of $E_{\mathbb Z}$; V2 ($d=2$) within $7.6\times10^{-12}$ of $E_{A_2}$: pass. \textbf{V3
($d=3$, $\alpha=\pi$) failed}: certified $B=0.2232177894732$, $3.05\times10^{-8}$ below the published $0.22321782$, outside the
registered $10^{-8}$ window. By the registered stop rule the $\alpha=2,4,8$ runs were reported as gated only, and the cause was left
undetermined. A machine reboot killed seven running jobs after about $6.5$\,h; the thirteen unfinished runs were relaunched
unchanged (no result was selected between launches), which is why the next round's driver checkpoints.

\textbf{H1$'''$: larger $K$ only} (\texttt{three\_d/H1ppp/}, pre-registration and frozen code \texttt{1e50f4b}). The method,
row grids, certificate, $b$ values and gate window of H1$''$ are unchanged; only $K=50$ (gate) and $K=60$ (V3 only, reported).
The registered evaluator turned out to be an unmodified copy of H1$''$'s, which reads only $K=40/30$ records; a replacement
applying the same criteria was committed (\texttt{162dfc3}) while the runs were in flight and before any record existed, after
intermediate per-round values in the logs had been seen (\texttt{H1ppp/DEVIATIONS.md}). The five runs ran uninterrupted
(about 10\,h at most). Certificates: $\tau\le1.1\times10^{-13}$, Gaussian lift $\delta\le2\times10^{-28}$. In three of the five
runs the vectors of the last 4--5 rounds could not be certified ($\hat f$ check failed for every $\lambda$); the reported values come
from rounds 13, 17, 13, 13 and 11, by which point the LP objective had stopped changing.

\textbf{A defect in the certificate code, found by review.} The hostile review of this version found that the frozen
certificate (\texttt{rig2.py}/\texttt{rig3.py}) does not check $\hat f\ge0$ between near-double real roots of $Q$ (pairs
$2\times10^{-15}$ to $3\times10^{-14}$ apart, with $Q<0$ between them) whose enclosures round to the same or adjacent
doubles, and that its bisection boxes, centred at floating-point midpoints, do not cover very narrow intervals exactly. An
independent re-check with exact box covering, outward-rounded root enclosures merged into clusters and the exact sign of $Q$ in
every gap (\texttt{H1ppp/recertify.py}) confirms all fourteen H1$''$ certificates and the four H1$'''$ $K=50$ certificates with
their recorded $\lambda$ and lift, reproducing $B$ to about 20 digits. The $K=60$ record, which had no lift ($\delta=0$), is
\emph{not} a valid certificate as recorded: $Q$ is negative at exact rational points in 13 sub-ulp intervals (down to
$-9.9\times10^{3}$ near $t=210.5$), so $\hat f<0$ there. With $\delta=10^{-25}$ it is certified and $B$ does not change to 20
digits. ``Certified'' in this section therefore rests on the frozen certificate together with this re-check; the frozen code and
records are unchanged.

\textbf{Reading.} The gate passes at $K=50$ with a margin of $9.4\times10^{-9}$, so the $\alpha=2,4,8$ values are registered
results: \emph{certified} lower bounds on the lower Gaussian energy of every unit-density configuration in $\mathbb R^3$ (energy
per point summed over ordered pairs, as in~\cite[Def.~1.1]{CdCI16} and in our lattice sums), $0.48\%$, $6.7\%$ and $21.6\%$
below the best lattice. Certification makes the \emph{bound values} rigorous. It does not make rigorous the statement that
two-point bounds at this degree cannot settle the three-dimensional Gaussian-core question at $\alpha=2,4,8$: that would need
a certified \emph{upper} bound on the LP optimum (a dual certificate, with $b$ optimised as well), which we did not compute. That
statement remains numerical, as in version~2 (with the same caveat at $\alpha=2$: it assumes no non-lattice state more than about
$0.4\%$ below BCC); the registered reading of H1$''$, which called it certified, overstated what a lower bound can show. At
$\alpha=\pi$ the reference is $\min(E_{\rm FCC},E_{\rm BCC})=0.2315363$, slightly above the coexistence record $0.2315353$ quoted
above; this does not matter for the gate. The V3 value rises monotonically with $K$ ($+3.0\times10^{-8}$ from 40 to 50,
$+5.2\times10^{-9}$ from 50 to 60): raising $K$ alone, with $b$, rows and row margin fixed, is \emph{sufficient} to bring the
certified value into the window. It does not identify the \emph{cause} of H1$''$'s shortfall: H1$'$'s uncertified LP objective at
the same $K=40$ and $b$ was already $0.2232178249$, while H1$''$'s $K=40$ objective is $0.2232177895$, so H1$''$'s formulation
(tail rows, row margin) may cost as much as the degree does, or H1$'$ overshot; we did not separate these. The registered
prediction (gate passes, $G>10^{-3}$ at all three, values within $10^{-6}$ relative of H1$'$'s) failed in its last part at
$\alpha=8$: the certified value is $8.3\times10^{-6}$ below H1$'$'s uncertified LP objective, which most likely overshot (the
H1$''$ certified $K=40$ value is also about $9\times10^{-6}$ below it). Not shown: convergence in $K$, the best two-point bound
at $K=50$ ($b$ was not re-optimised), anything about three-point or semidefinite bounds.
