\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A quantized vortex in a thermal two-dimensional Bose gas is dragged by the normal fluid (friction $\alpha$),
possibly pushed sideways by it (transverse coefficient $\alpha'$) and kicked by it (diffusion $\eta$). Whether the
transverse force exists has been disputed for thirty years; the only first-principles values of $\alpha$ for a
bosonic classical field stop at $0.18\,T_{\rm BKT}$ and carry no error bar on $\alpha'$~\cite{Shukla2014}; the
fluctuation--dissipation relation between $\eta$ and $\alpha$ has never been tested in a closed system and was found
violated a hundredfold in one experiment~\cite{Neely2024}. We measure the three coefficients in the projected
Gross--Pitaevskii equation --- microcanonical, no damping term, no noise added --- by tracking imprinted vortex
pairs with sub-grid resolution and reading the coefficients through estimators that are exact identities of the
dissipative point-vortex model (machine-checked in Lean~4). At $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$, $0.27$ and $0.43$ we find
$\alpha=0.0062(4)$, $0.014(2)$ and $0.02(1)$, independent of pair size and of estimator, and proportional to the
normal fraction, $\alpha\simeq0.24\,\rho_n/\rho$. The transverse coefficient is zero within $1\%$ once the
kinematics of a compressible pair on a torus are accounted for; the Iordanskii value $\rho_n/\rho$ is excluded at
$18\sigma$. The vortex diffuses at $1.4$--$3$ times the Einstein value --- not $100$ times --- but the fluctuations are
not cleanly diffusive, so the relation is left undecided by our own rule. A single pair in a closed box stops
shrinking while the phonon band absorbs the impulse it sheds, a self-generated counterflow; \PENDING{the $L=96$
control decides whether the stall is the box's}. On the static side we show that the depressed stiffness
seen in finite-box simulations is one unscreened box-scale pair left over from relaxation, that it decays given
time that grows faster than $L^2$, and that the thermal vortex-pair gas has a wavevector-independent charge
structure factor equal to the Kosterlitz--Thouless polarisability computed from the measured pair sizes to
$5$--$21\%$ --- while a mode-by-mode dielectric relation between current and vortex charge, which we had
registered as a prediction, fails. Every hypothesis, control and decision rule was committed before the data it
governs existed, and the failures are reported with the results (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

With the normal fluid at rest, a quantized vortex of charge $q$ moves at
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf v=(1-\alpha')\,\mathbf v_s-\alpha\,q\,\hat z\times\mathbf v_s ,
  \label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf v_s$ is the superfluid velocity at its position induced by all other vortices
($\hbar=m=1$, circulation $2\pi$)~\cite{Iordanskii1964,Sonin1997}. The longitudinal coefficient $\alpha$ is the
mutual friction; $\alpha'$ measures the transverse force exerted by the normal component. For a weakly dissipative
vortex the two sides of a long controversy predict different $\alpha'$: no transverse force from the normal fluid,
$\alpha'=0$~\cite{ThoulessAoNiu1996}, or the Iordanskii force, $\alpha'=\rho_n/\rho$~\cite{Iordanskii1964,Sonin1997}.
If the field around the vortex is also its heat bath, the random kicks it receives are tied to the drag by the
fluctuation--dissipation (Einstein) relation $\eta=\alpha T/(2\pi\rho)$ ($k_B=1$)~\cite{Mehdi2023}.

For a bosonic classical field these quantities are poorly known. Shukla, Brachet and Pandit measured $\alpha$ in the
truncated Gross--Pitaevskii equation only below $0.18\,T_{\rm BKT}$ and could not resolve the sign of
$\alpha'$~\cite{Shukla2014}; in three-dimensional Kelvin-wave fits $\alpha'$ ``deviates'' from
expectation~\cite{KrstulovicBrachet2023}; the Einstein relation has never been tested in a closed field, helium-film
analyses assume it~\cite{AHNS1980}, and the one cold-atom measurement of $\eta$ exceeds the prediction by two
orders of magnitude, attributed to trap-wall vibrations~\cite{Neely2024}. A review is~\cite{Sergeev2023}. A closed,
energy-conserving classical field has no walls, no reservoir and no noise put in by hand: whatever drag and
diffusion a vortex experiences there come from the field's own thermal phonons.

A second, static question concerns how the same thermal field screens vortices. The superfluid stiffness is read
from the transverse current response, $\rho_n(k)=\langle|J_T(k)|^2\rangle/(TL^2)$; in the Coulomb-gas picture bound
vortex pairs reduce it through their polarisation~\cite{KosterlitzThouless1973,Kosterlitz1974,VallatBeck1994,Faulkner2017}.
For a compressible Bose field no functional form at finite wavevector exists: classical-field studies extrapolate the
response to $k\to0$ by fits of convenience and have reported negative superfluid fractions attributed to
noise~\cite{Foster2010,GawrylukBrewczyk2019}. We had found earlier that such negative values are carried by one
non-equilibrium vortex pair of box size, and had registered as a prediction that the transverse current is, mode by
mode, the point-vortex current of the detected vortex charges. That prediction is tested here and fails; what
replaces it is a direct measurement of the pair gas's polarisability from vortex positions.

The study was run as a pre-registered programme: each hypothesis, each control with its pass threshold, and each
decision rule was committed to the repository before the data it governs existed, and the controls that failed are
reported alongside the results. The estimators used on the data are identities of the dissipative point-vortex
model; we proved them in the Lean~4 proof assistant so that what is assumed about the model is exactly what is
stated (Section~\ref{sec:estimators}). Section~\ref{sec:model} describes the model and the instrument,
Section~\ref{sec:transport} the transport coefficients, Section~\ref{sec:wind} the behaviour of a single pair in a
closed box, Section~\ref{sec:static} the box-scale pair, equilibration and the polarisability of the pair gas, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} what is established. The protocol, with every gate and amendment, is
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}; the corrected vortex imprint is Appendix~\ref{app:imprint}; the theorems are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:lean}.

\section{Model, instrument and estimators}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Field.} We integrate the projected Gross--Pitaevskii equation
$i\partial_t\psi=\mathcal P[-\tfrac12\nabla^2\psi+g|\psi|^2\psi]$ on a square periodic box with $\hbar=m=g=1$,
mean density $n=1$ (healing length $\xi=1$, sound speed $c=1$), grid spacing $\xi/2$ and a circular momentum cutoff
at half the grid wavenumber; the projector $\mathcal P$ keeps the dynamics inside the cutoff. Energy is conserved to
$10^{-7}$--$10^{-6}$ over every run. Thermal states are prepared by relaxing a random high-energy field at the target
energy and, for the warmer states, by adding phonons to a colder state and equilibrating; their temperature comes
from a two-window equipartition thermometer and their superfluid fraction from the current correlators, both
validated in earlier rounds of this programme; $T_{\rm BKT}(L{=}64)=0.821$ from a heating ladder. Transport runs use
$L=64$ at $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$, $0.27$, $0.43$ (no thermal vortex in the base state, or fewer than $0.4$ on average)
and one report-only state at $0.56$ with thermal pairs; the static questions use $L=192$ at $T/T_{\rm BKT}\approx0.6$--$0.85$.

\paragraph{Imprinting a pair.} A vortex pair is written into a thermal state by multiplying the field by a
theta-function phase and an amplitude factor. Two defects of the imprint used in the earlier rounds of this programme
were found by the $T=0$ control of this study and corrected (Appendix~\ref{app:imprint}): the theta-function phase of
a single pair is periodic only up to a constant jump $2\pi d/L$ across one boundary of the box, which we remove by a
uniform phase gradient; and the customary per-vortex amplitude $[r^2/(r^2+2)]^{1/2}$ has a $1/r^2$ tail four times
the quasi-static (Bernoulli) depletion, whose excess leaves the box as sound and pushes the vortices. We use instead
the Bernoulli amplitude $[1+|\mathbf v|^2/2]^{-1/2}$ built from the velocity of the imprinted phase itself. Figure~\ref{fig:fields} shows the
two imprints side by side. With the old imprint a pair at $T=0$ drifted by $0.6$--$0.8$ in separation over $400$ time units --- an apparent friction as
large as the thermal one at $0.14\,T_{\rm BKT}$ (cf.~\cite{Rorai2013} on product-ansatz pairs); with the corrected one
it does not move (below).

\paragraph{Geometry.} A pair of separation $d$ carries the impulse $2\pi\rho_sd$. In a periodic box that impulse
cannot be shed without consequence (Section~\ref{sec:wind}), so transport runs use two antiparallel pairs
(a $+-$ pair at $x=L/4$ and a $-+$ pair at $x=3L/4$, same separation, moving in opposite directions): zero net
charge, zero net dipole moment, zero net impulse. Single pairs are used only for the question of
Section~\ref{sec:wind}.

\paragraph{Detection and tracking.} Vortices are located by the phase winding around each plaquette (the rule
proved correct in \lean{VortexWinding.lean}) and refined to sub-grid precision by the zero of the least-squares
plane through the four corner values of $\psi$: $0.01$ at $T=0$, with a thermal jitter of $\approx0.3$ at
$T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$. The imprinted vortices are followed by continuity (same sign, nearest detection within $3.0$
per time unit), positions are saved every time unit, and a track is used only while every vortex--antivortex
distance exceeds $4\xi$, below which a pair is a single solitary wave rather than two vortices.

\paragraph{Estimators (identities of the model).}\label{sec:estimators} Let $H$ be the point-vortex Hamiltonian of the
tracked configuration on the torus (the Weiss--McWilliams sum~\cite{WeissMcWilliams1991}). Equation~\eqref{eq:motion}
for all vortices is a skew part --- the conservative flow, which also contains $\alpha'$ --- plus
$-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\nabla H$, so that $\dot H=-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla H|^2=-2\alpha\sum_i|\mathbf v_{s,i}|^2$ for
any configuration and any $\alpha'$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{\rm energy}=-\frac{\Delta H}{2\int\sum_i|\mathbf v_{s,i}|^2\,dt},
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
which needs no pairing of vortices and is blind to $\alpha'$. Independently, the displacement of each vortex over a
lag regressed on the two orthogonal predictors $\int\mathbf v_{s,i}\,dt$ and $-q_i\!\int\hat z\times\mathbf v_{s,i}\,dt$
gives $1-\alpha'$ and $\alpha$ as the two coefficients; the mean square of the residuals of that regression against
lag, $\langle|e|^2\rangle=c_0+4\eta\tau$, gives the diffusion constant, which we quote only if the local scaling
exponent on lags $20$--$400$ lies in $[0.8,1.2]$. For a stochastic pair with drift $-2\alpha\mathbf d/|\mathbf d|^2$
and isotropic diffusion $D=2\eta$ of the separation, the Kosterlitz--Thouless pair distribution $|\mathbf d|^{-K}$,
$K=2\pi\rho_s/T$, carries zero probability current if and only if $DK=2\alpha$, i.e.\ $\eta=\alpha T/(2\pi\rho_s)$:
the Einstein statistic is $R_E=\eta K/\alpha$, equal to one when the pair gas can be in detailed balance at the
measured stiffness. These statements --- the dissipation identity, the $d^2$ law of a plane pair and its lifetime
bound, the centre-velocity identity behind the $\alpha'$ estimator, and the zero-current characterisation of the
Einstein relation --- are theorems in Lean~4 (Appendix~\ref{app:lean}): the proof assistant checks, from the model's
equations alone, that the quantities we compute from positions are what we say they are, with no hidden assumption.

\paragraph{Controls.} The estimators recover known $(\alpha,\alpha',\eta)$ from synthetic Langevin trajectories to
$7\%$, $0.002$ and $25\%$. At $T=0$ (uniform condensate), with the corrected imprint, a pair of separation $9.8$
neither shrinks nor diffuses over $400$ time units ($|\alpha|\le4\times10^{-4}$, residual mean-square displacement
bounded at $0.005$), its members move at the torus point-vortex velocity to $0.14\%$, and a single pair's field
momentum is constant to $3\times10^{-10}$ and equal to $0.96$--$0.99$ of $2\pi nd$ --- consistent with the
theoretical expectation that an isolated pair does not shrink at $T=0$~\cite{LucasSurowka2014}. At
$T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$ two antiparallel pairs give $\alpha=0.00705\pm0.00012$, inside a window set before the run
from~\cite{Shukla2014}. Full gate record: Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\paragraph{Friction.} Figure~\ref{fig:alpha} (left) and Table~\ref{tab:alpha} give $\alpha$ at the three
temperatures from six runs each (pair sizes $8$ and $12$, three seeds, $2000$ time units; eight runs at the coldest).
The three criteria registered for it pass: the two pair sizes agree (ratios $0.91$, $0.76$, $1.04$, within the

$13$ and $18\%$. The warmest base contains a thermal vortex in more than $5\%$ of its samples and its error is
large; it is kept with that flag. What was not registered and is the cleanest statement of the data is the ratio
$\alpha/(\rho_n/\rho)=0.23$, $0.26$, $0.22$: within errors, \textbf{the friction is proportional to the normal
fraction, $\alpha\simeq0.24\,\rho_n/\rho$}, over a factor $3.5$ in $\rho_n$. At $0.14\,T_{\rm BKT}$ our $0.0062$
is a factor two below the interpolation of~\cite{Shukla2014}, whose transition temperature is itself a rough
estimate; at $0.27$ and $0.43$ no closed-field value existed, and ours lie in the range of the oblate-condensate
measurements~\cite{Moon2015} and above the unitary-gas value~\cite{Kwon2021}. An independent configuration of this
programme (eight imprinted vortices at $d=16$, $0.14\,T_{\rm BKT}$) gives $\alpha=0.0048$--$0.0057$ with the same
estimators.

\paragraph{Transverse coefficient, and the frame in which it is defined.} The regression gives $1-\alpha'$
directly; Table~\ref{tab:alpha} lists the values obtained in the frame of the simulation box. They are
\emph{negative}: the tracked vortices move $1$--$1.5\%$ faster than the point-vortex velocity induced by their
partners, while at $T=0$ they move $0.14\%$ slower. Neither theory has that sign, and the effect differs between the
two pair sizes at the same temperature ($-0.0135\pm0.0018$ at $d_0=8$, $-0.0038\pm0.0016$ at $12$), which points to a
kinematic rather than a thermal origin. Two kinematic facts about a compressible pair on a torus must be accounted
for before Eq.~\eqref{eq:motion} is read, and both are measured, not assumed.

First, the velocity $\mathbf v_s$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:motion} is the \emph{actual} superfluid velocity at the vortex,
and on a torus that includes the uniform part of the flow. A configuration with net dipole moment $\mathbf D=\sum q\mathbf r$
carries the field momentum $2\pi n\,\hat z\times\mathbf D$, which in a periodic box is a mean superfluid velocity
$\mathbf u=\mathbf P/(nL^2)$ of the whole fluid; the Weiss--McWilliams Green function, like any periodic point-vortex
construction, fixes that uniform component by a gauge choice and not by the configuration's momentum. The physical
transverse force is a force exerted by the normal fluid, which in a closed box is at rest in the box frame, on a
vortex moving relative to it; its signature is a difference between the vortex velocity and the \emph{total} local
superfluid velocity, $\mathbf v_s^{\rm WM}+\mathbf u$. Omitting $\mathbf u$ makes a single pair appear to move faster
than $\mathbf v_s$ by $|\mathbf u|d=2\pi d^2/L^2$ --- $6\%$ at $d=6$ and $49\%$ at $d=16$ in a $T=0$ sweep of single
pairs, and exactly what subtracting $\mathbf u$ removes (Table~\ref{tab:T0}). For the antiparallel geometry
$|\mathbf u|\le2\times10^{-3}$ and the two pairs move in opposite directions, so this correction to the production
values is below $0.0002$; it is decisive only for single pairs.

Second, a pair of finite size is not two point vortices: it is a solitary wave of the compressible field (the
Jones--Roberts family), whose speed departs from $1/d$ by a correction of order $(\xi/d)^2$. That correction is a
property of the $T=0$ field, independent of any normal fluid, so it must be measured at $T=0$ and subtracted. In the
fluid frame a $T=0$ pair moves at the torus point-vortex speed to $+0.4\%$ for $d\ge8$, $+1\%$ at $d=6$ and
$+3$--$4\%$ at $d=3$--$4$ (Table~\ref{tab:T0}). The thermal fits of Table~\ref{tab:alpha} use pairs of mean matched
size $8.7$--$9.3$ but weight the smaller, faster ones more, so their $T=0$ baseline is $1-\alpha'_{T=0}\approx1.004$
to $1.01$.

After both corrections the thermal transverse coefficient is $\alpha'=-0.006\pm0.002$, $-0.011\pm0.003$ and
$-0.006\pm0.011$ at the three temperatures, with an uncertainty of the same order from the mapping between fit
weights and pair sizes: \textbf{$\alpha'$ is zero within about $1\%$}, Figure~\ref{fig:alpha} (right). The
decision rule registered for this quantity was a weighted regression of $\alpha'$ on $\rho_n/\rho$ including the
$T=0$ point; it gives slope $b=-0.31\pm0.05$ in the box frame (and a slope compatible with zero after the baseline),
against the Iordanskii window $b\in[0.6,1.4]$ and the no-transverse-force criterion ``$2\sigma$ upper bound on $b$
below $0.3$''. \textbf{The registered prediction --- Iordanskii-like --- is refuted at $18\sigma$}; the Iordanskii values
$+2.7\%$, $+5.3\%$ and $+9.4\%$ at the three temperatures are excluded, and the rule returns ``no transverse force''.
The eight-vortex configuration at $d=16$ gives $1-\alpha'=1.0012$, consistent. We note that the measurement
is of a classical Rayleigh--Jeans field at one cutoff; the phonon scattering that would produce an Iordanskii force in
a quantum gas has its classical counterpart here, and that counterpart does not produce one at the $1\%$ level in
this regime.

\paragraph{Diffusion and the Einstein relation.} Figure~\ref{fig:msd} shows the residual mean-square displacement
against lag. At $T=0$ it is bounded at $0.005$: the residual is the vortex riding on the sound that remains in the
periodic box. At $T>0$ it has a short-lag plateau of $0.2$--$1.2$ --- the thermal jitter of the detected core position,
whose correlation time is that of the phonons --- followed by growth. The local exponent on lags $20$--$400$ is $0.71$,
$0.82$ and $1.28$ at the three temperatures; on lags $\ge100$ the two cooler ones are Brownian ($1.1$--$1.2$).
Extracting a diffusion constant from a sub-diffusive window is ill-posed, which is why the rule registered before the
analysis quotes $\eta$ only where the exponent lies in $[0.8,1.2]$: that admits the middle temperature alone,
$\eta=(1.08\pm0.35)\times10^{-3}$, giving $R_E=\eta K/\alpha=2.1\pm0.7$; the two unquoted temperatures would give
$3.2$ and $1.4$. \textbf{By the registered rule the Einstein relation is inconclusive} (two admitted temperatures were
required). What the data say informally is that the vortex diffuses at $1.4$--$3$ times the Einstein value, the
excess decreasing with temperature --- far from the hundredfold of~\cite{Neely2024}, and not cleanly one. The
sub-diffusion at the coldest temperature is a short-lag regime, not a bounded orbit: a finite quasi-periodic sum has
a bounded mean-square increment, so growth refutes a regular island while a plateau never proves one
(\lean{QuasiPeriodicBound.lean}), and the spectrum of a stalled pair's separation (next section) is a $1/f^{0.5}$
continuum, not lines. The autocorrelation of the residual velocity at $0.14\,T_{\rm BKT}$ has a small negative lobe,
$-0.010\pm0.003$, at lag $79$ --- near the acoustic crossing time of the box, $L/c=64$ --- which is at least
consistent with the vortex meeting its own shed sound. The physical reading we can defend is that at the lowest temperature the vortex's bath is
not yet Markovian on the lag scale of the fit: the kicks come from phonons whose period ($L/c\approx64$) is not short
compared with the lags, and the thermal vortices that make the warmest state Brownian also add drift.

\section{A single pair in a closed box: the phonon wind}\label{sec:wind}

The impulse $2\pi\rho_sd$ of a shrinking pair has to go somewhere. In a periodic box it goes to the phonons, which
then drift at $u=2\pi\rho_s(d_0-d)/(\rho_nL^2)$ in the direction of the pair's motion, and the friction, which acts on
the velocity of the vortex \emph{relative to the normal fluid}, is reduced: $\dot d=-2\alpha(1/d-u)$. When
$\pi\rho_sd_0^2>2\rho_nL^2$ the right-hand side has a zero at $b=[d_0+(d_0^2-2\rho_nL^2/\pi\rho_s)^{1/2}]/2$ and the pair
stalls there instead of annihilating (a theorem of the reduced equation, \lean{wind\_stall}). At $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$,
$L=64$, a pair of $d_0=12$ should stall ($b=10.2$ in the plane formula, $\approx9$ with the torus image forces) and
a pair of $d_0=8$ should not; in an $L=96$ box neither should.

Figures~\ref{fig:wind} and~\ref{fig:portrait} show two single-pair runs of $4000$ time units against the
zero-impulse pairs of the same temperature. The zero-impulse pairs shrink steadily from $10$ to $7$--$8$ within $1500$ time units; neither single
pair annihilates. One shrinks from $11.6$ to $9.3$ by $t\approx1800$ and stays there for the remaining $2200$ time
units (the late slope of $d^2$ is $4\%$ of the early one); the other shrinks to $10.2$ and re-expands to $11.2$,
moving back toward the predicted stall point. In both, the momentum carried by the modes with $|k|>1$ follows the
impulse shed by the pair --- slopes $0.68$ and $0.52$ against the registered window $[0.5,1.2]$ --- and falls back when
the pair re-expands. The object has a published precedent: in the truncated GPE a thermal cloud with net momentum
relative to the condensate is a metastable counterflow decaying only by activated ring
nucleation~\cite{KrstulovicBrachet2011}; here the counterflow is generated by the pair itself. On the torus a single
zero-circulation pair is a relative equilibrium of the point-vortex flow~\cite{ModinViviani2020}, so the wobble of the
stalled pair is bath-driven; its spectrum over $t\in[2000,4000]$ is a $1/f^{0.5}$ continuum with no dominant line.

The decisive control is the same pair in an $L=96$ box at the same temperature ($T=0.110$, $\rho_n/\rho=0.029$,
no thermal vortex), where $\rho_nL^2/(2\pi\rho_s)=44>d_0^2/4=36$ and the plane criterion predicts no stall.
The registered reading of the control was binary --- a pair that keeps shrinking supports the mechanism, a pair that
stalls near the $L=64$ separation refutes it --- with a tie-break of two further runs if two runs disagree.
\PENDING{W2 verdict on the four L = 96 runs: first two disagree (one keeps shrinking, late slope five times the
early one; one shows a stall-like plateau near 9.6); runs 3 and 4 integrating}. What the control shows beyond the
binary reading is in Figure~\ref{fig:wind} (left): integrating the wind equation with the friction measured on
the zero-impulse pairs, $\alpha=0.0062$, and \emph{no free parameter}, predicts at $L=64$ a stall near $10$ and at
$L=96$ not a stall but a drive halved once the pair has shed two units of separation; the measured pairs end
within about one unit of that curve at both sizes and three to five units above free shrinking
($d^2=d_0^2-4\alpha t$), which would have taken them to $5$--$6$. The suppression is graded with box size, as the
mechanism says, and the binary criterion was too coarse an instrument for a graded effect; the stall map of the
follow-up pre-registration (eleven cells in $L$, $d_0$ and $T$) is the quantitative test. If the mechanism holds,
every friction measurement on a single pair in a closed box at low temperature is contaminated unless
$\rho_nL^2\gg\pi\rho_sd_0^2/2$ --- a condition that the truncated-GPE measurements of the literature should be checked
against, and the reason the production runs of Section~\ref{sec:transport} use a
zero-impulse geometry.

\section{The box-scale pair, equilibration, and the polarisability of the pair gas}\label{sec:static}

\paragraph{One unscreened pair.} In an earlier round, seven of eighteen large-box runs ($L=128$, $192$) reached a
negative superfluid fraction with the same number of vortices as healthy partners at the same energy. A
pre-registered test on their vortex positions refuted the natural suspicion of Onsager clustering: no like-sign
clustering, positive vortex temperature, $98$--$99.7\%$ of vortices paired with an opposite-sign neighbour. What
distinguished them, found after the fact, was small and specific: the transverse current that point vortices drive,
$(2\pi)^2|\rho_q(k)|^2/k^2$ with $\rho_q(k)=\sum_jq_je^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r_j}$, computed from the positions alone
at the three longest wavelengths, tracks the measured transverse current across all eighteen runs with $r=0.996$.
The anomaly is one $O(1)$ unscreened pair of box size among hundreds of bound ones --- the finite-$k$ form of the
polarisation term of~\cite{VallatBeck1994,Faulkner2017}, and the surviving post-quench pair that Chu and Williams
asked simulations to look for~\cite{ChuWilliams2001}. Its Boltzmann weight, $(L/\xi)^{-K}\lesssim10^{-9}$, makes it
a remnant of the initial relaxation, not an equilibrium feature; equilibrium finite-size stiffness is monotone in
$L$~\cite{ProkofevSvistunov2002,Hasenbusch2005}.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/vt_fig5_equilibration.pdf}
\caption{The six $L=192$ trajectories at two sampling windows, $t\in[6000,7000]$ and, after continuing them,
$t\in[13\,500,14\,500]$: the vortex-driven transverse response at the longest wavelength (left, logarithmic; the
dotted line is the registered threshold $0.45$) and the superfluid fraction from the transverse estimator (right).
Starred runs are the two that had a negative superfluid fraction.}
\label{fig:equil}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Equilibration.} Coarsening under conservative dynamics has a dynamic exponent $z\approx1.7$--$1.8$ in
this model~\cite{GroszekBillam2026} ($L^2\ln L$ for dissipative XY dynamics~\cite{Bray2000,JelicCugliandolo2011};
$L^2/\ln L$ for Hamiltonian XY~\cite{Nam2012}), so the warm-up that had cured the $L=128$ states should scale to
$11\,000$--$14\,600$ time units at $L=192$. We registered, before the runs, that continuing the six $L=192$ trajectories
to $t=14\,500$ should admit at least five of six by the isotropy criterion, bring the vortex transverse response below
$0.45$ in every admitted run, and reduce it by at least a factor two in the two anomalous ones. Figure~\ref{fig:equil}:
all six are admitted (three were), the anomalous runs drop by factors $27$ and $4.2$, and five of six are below the
threshold --- the sixth, still at $0.80$ with $K=3.5$ against its partner's $8.3$, has not finished. The box-scale
charge decays as the mechanism says in every run, and the equilibration time of an $L=192$ box at this temperature
exceeds $13\,500$ for some trajectories: longer than the $L^2$ and $z\approx1.7$ estimates, as the $L^2\ln L$ form would
have it, on the evidence of one trajectory. On the equilibrated window the duality offsets $\eta_{g_1}K-1$ are $+0.20$,
$+0.16$ and $+0.30$ at the three energies: positive at every box size, so an earlier reading of these runs as showing
a saturating finite-size offset rested on under-equilibrated states.

\paragraph{The Josephson offset.} On the same equilibrated states the coherence exponent fitted with the exact torus
Green function on $r\in[8,32]$ gives $\eta_{g_1}K-1=+0.31\pm0.11$, unchanged by the fit window and equal to the
naive-window value: the $12$--$27\%$ excess over the Josephson relation reported at $L\le64$ is neither a geometry
factor (cf.\ ``close to unity''~\cite{HolzmannBaym2007}) nor a finite-size transient. It persists at $L=192$ in
equilibrium. One candidate can already be excluded: that the offset is the signature of amplitude (density) fluctuations in
$g_1=\langle\psi^*\psi\rangle$. The phase-only correlator $\langle e^{i(\theta-\theta')}\rangle$, fitted on the same
states, the same windows and the same torus Green function, carries \emph{exactly} the same offset ($+0.225$ against
$+0.225$ on $r\in[8,48]$; difference $0.000\pm0.001$ over the six runs). The offset is not in the amplitude of
$g_1$. What remains is the estimator of $K$ itself --- the transverse current response at the lowest wavevectors of
a fixed-momentum microcanonical field, for which no fluctuation formula has been derived --- or the relation as
applied at this coupling ($mg=1$). Reported, not explained.

\includegraphics[width=0.52\textwidth]{figures/vt_fig4_structure_factor.pdf}
\caption{Vortex-charge structure factor of the six equilibrated $L=192$ states, $\langle|\rho_q(k)|^2\rangle/k^2$ on
the shells $|m|^2=1$--$16$, divided by the dipole polarisability $\Sigma_{\rm pairs}d^2/2$ computed from the

\paragraph{The dielectric relation that fails, and the polarisability that holds.} We had registered, before the
$L=192$ states above existed, that the transverse current is, mode by mode and snapshot by snapshot, the point-vortex
current of the detected charges plus a phonon part uncorrelated with them: a coherence $\gamma^2\ge0.4$ between
$J_T(k,t)$ and $-2\pi i\rho_q(k,t)/|k|$ on every shell up to $|m|^2=16$ in at least five of six runs, with one real
$k$-independent coefficient. Five controls on imprinted pair gases had failed on the amplitude's expected value
(a tight pair carries less current than two point vortices, $0.92$, $0.97$, $0.99$ at $d=5,8,14$) while the
coherence criterion passed in all of them --- $\gamma^2=1.000$ at $T=0$. On the six equilibrated thermal states,
with $170$--$830$ vortices almost all in tight pairs, \textbf{the coherence is $0.06$--$0.28$}: the criterion fails in
five of six, and the one run that passes is the one still carrying a box-scale pair. The phonon normal fraction,
$f\approx0.16$--$0.26$, is the larger part of the transverse response there, and whatever the tight pairs contribute is
not coherent with their instantaneous charge density at that wavevector. The $r=0.996$ of the diagnosis above was,
as the pre-registration itself warned, a correlation of run-averaged powers carried by the box-scale pairs. The
diagnostic detects one unscreened pair; it is not a dielectric function of the pair gas.

What does hold is the static statement, Figure~\ref{fig:sq}: in the five runs without a box-scale pair the charge
structure factor $\langle|\rho_q(k)|^2\rangle/k^2$ is independent of $k$ out to $k\xi\approx0.13$, and its value is the
dipole polarisability $\Sigma_{\rm pairs}d^2/2$ computed from the matched pair sizes, to $5$--$21\%$ and always below
it. This is the Kosterlitz--Thouless polarisability $\varepsilon-1\propto\Sigma d^2$~\cite{KosterlitzThouless1973,Kosterlitz1974}
read directly from vortex positions in a Bose field, with a factor $0.8$--$0.95$ that we read as the screening of
dipoles by dipoles; the pair-size law itself had been measured before only in the XY model~\cite{FanWilliams2022}. How
much of the stiffness that polarisability accounts for is what the failure of the mode-by-mode relation leaves open:
the current carried by a thermal pair of size $1$--$3\xi$ is not the point-vortex current, and we do not yet have its
form factor.

\section{What this establishes, and what it does not}\label{sec:conclusions}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Established} (pre-registered criteria passed, or controls stated as such).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A vortex pair at $T=0$ in the projected GPE neither shrinks nor diffuses and moves at the torus point-vortex
  velocity to $0.14\%$ in the fluid frame; tight pairs ($d\le6$) move $1$--$4\%$ faster, a compressible correction.
\item $\alpha=0.0062(4)$, $0.014(2)$, $0.02(1)$ at $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$, $0.27$, $0.43$, independent of pair size and
  estimator; $\alpha\simeq0.24\,\rho_n/\rho$.
\item $\alpha'$ is zero within about $1\%$ after the $T=0$ kinematics; the Iordanskii value is excluded at $18\sigma$.
\item The vortex diffuses at $1.4$--$3$ times the Einstein value, not $100$; the relation itself is undecided by our rule.
\item The depressed stiffness of finite-box classical-field simulations is one unscreened box-scale pair, not
  clustering; given time growing faster than $L^2$ it decays (all six $L=192$ runs admitted; one not finished).
\item The thermal pair gas has a $k$-independent charge structure factor equal to the Kosterlitz--Thouless
  polarisability from the measured pair sizes to $5$--$21\%$.
\item The Josephson offset $\eta_{g_1}K-1=+0.31\pm0.11$ survives at $L=192$ in equilibrium, independent of window and
  torus geometry.
\item The two imprint defects (phase seam, $1/r^2$ amplitude tail) and their correction.
\end{itemize}
\end{keybox}
\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Refuted, undecided, or open.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Refuted:} the registered Iordanskii-like prediction; the mode-by-mode dielectric relation in the thermal pair
  gas; the Onsager-cluster reading of the depressed-stiffness states; the earlier ``saturating offset'' reading.
\item \emph{Undecided by rule:} the Einstein relation (one admitted temperature).
\item \emph{Open:} the phonon-wind stall \PENDING{until the $L=96$ control}; the cause of the Josephson offset; the current
  form factor of tight thermal pairs; why $\alpha\propto\rho_n$ with coefficient $0.24$.
\item \emph{Scope:} a Rayleigh--Jeans field with one momentum cutoff, $mg=1$, two dimensions; the classical-field normal
  fraction depends on the cutoff, so comparisons with quantum gases are made at equal $\rho_n/\rho$, not equal $T/T_c$.
\end{itemize}
\end{failbox}

\paragraph{On method.} The point-vortex model is a variational projection of the field onto the manifold of vortex
positions, and the coefficients measured here are the statistics of the field's leak out of that manifold --- the
same object that a recent many-body experiment reads from the other end, by projecting a quantum state back onto a
variational manifold at every step and asking how much coherence survives~\cite{Dong2026}. The hypotheses tested here
were ranked by an automated five-minute-probe loop under priors fixed before any probe ran, from a local literature
database of $774$ entries whose $382$ arXiv identifiers were verified against the arXiv API; the protocol, the gates
that failed and the one verdict that was withdrawn are in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\section{The corrected imprint}\label{app:imprint}

For vortices of charge $q_j=\pm1$ at $\mathbf r_j=(x_j,y_j)$ in the square box of side $L$, the phase factor is
built from the Jacobi function $\vartheta_1(u\,|\,\tau=i)$ with $u_j=\pi[(x-x_j)+i(y-y_j)]/L$:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_0(\mathbf r)=\prod_j\Big(\frac{\vartheta_1(u_j)}{|\vartheta_1(u_j)|}\Big)^{q_j}.
\end{equation}
Under $y\to y+L$ each factor picks up $e^{-2i\,q_j\pi(x-x_j)/L}$, so for $\sum q_j=0$ the product is periodic in $y$
only up to the constant phase $2\pi D_x/L$, $D_x=\sum_jq_jx_j$ (and periodic in $x$). A single pair of separation $d$
along $x$ therefore carries a phase step $2\pi d/L$ along the whole line $y=0$, which the projection turns into a
sheet of sound. The corrected phase removes the jumps measured on $\Phi_0$ itself,
$\varphi_x=\arg\langle\Phi_0(\mathbf r+L\hat x)\overline{\Phi_0(\mathbf r)}\rangle$ and likewise $\varphi_y$, by the
uniform gradient (the torus counterflow of the configuration, in the minimal sector):
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\mathbf r)=\Phi_0(\mathbf r)\,e^{-i(\varphi_xx+\varphi_yy)/L}.
\end{equation}
The amplitude is the quasi-static (Bernoulli) density of the imprinted flow, $\mathbf v=\nabla\arg\Phi$ evaluated by
centred differences of $\Phi$ on the grid:
\begin{equation}
  \psi\;\to\;\mathcal P\Big[\psi\,\Phi\,\big(1+\tfrac12|\mathbf v|^2\big)^{-1/2}\Big]\times\text{(norm restored)}.
\end{equation}
This vanishes at the cores, approaches $1-|\mathbf v|^2/2$ far from them, and for a neutral configuration removes a
density that falls off as the pair's own velocity field, $|\mathbf v|^2\propto d^2/r^4$ --- against the $1/r^2$ tail of
the per-vortex factor $[r^2/(r^2+2)]^{1/2}$, which removes $21\%$ of the density of a $64^2$ box for $24$ vortices
before the norm is restored and launches the corresponding sound. Static check: a single pair of $d=10$ has field
momentum $60.2$ against $2\pi nd=62.8$ (was $49.8$ with the seam) and peak density $1.007$ (was $1.087$).
Dynamic check: Section~\ref{sec:model}, controls.

\section{Machine-checked statements}\label{app:lean}

All modules are in \texttt{lean\_src/}, checked by the Lean~4 kernel against Mathlib with the standard axioms only
(\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}). What is proved is that the estimators and the reduced
equations used in the text are consequences of the model's equations, not that the model describes the field.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \lean{DissipativeVortexDynamics}: for any flow $\dot{\mathbf r}=A(\nabla H)-\gamma\nabla H$ with $A$ pointwise
  skew, $\tfrac{d}{dt}H=-\gamma|\nabla H|^2$ (the energy estimator, independent of the skew part and hence of $\alpha'$);
  for the plane pair, $|\mathbf d|^2=|\mathbf d_0|^2-4\alpha t$ on the interval of existence, the lifetime bound, and the
  centre velocity $(1-\alpha')/|\mathbf d|$ perpendicular to the axis; for $\dot d=-c(d-a)(d-b)/d$ with $0<a<b$, a pair
  starting at or above $b$ never falls below it (the wind stall).
\item \lean{EinsteinRelation}: the pair distribution $|\mathbf d|^{-K}$ carries zero Fokker--Planck current for drift
  $-2\alpha\mathbf d/|\mathbf d|^2$ and diffusion $D$ iff $DK=2\alpha$; in vortex units $\eta=\alpha T/(2\pi\rho)$.
\item \lean{PairPolarisation}: $\|\rho_q(k)\|\le\|k\|\sum_i\|\mathbf d_i\|$; $\rho_q(k)=i\,\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf P(k)$ with
  remainder $\le\sum_i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf d_i)^2$ when $|\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf d_i|\le1$; a $k$-independent ceiling of the
  bound-pair transverse response.
\item \lean{MatchingScreening}: $\|\rho_q(k)\|\le\|k\|\times$(the length of any vortex--antivortex matching), on the plane
  and the torus; the Fourier velocity of point vortices is transverse with $\|\hat v\|^2=(2\pi)^2\|\rho_q\|^2/\|k\|^2$.
\item \lean{KTFlow}: the exact invariant $2u-\pi\ln u-2\pi^3y^2$ of the Kosterlitz flow and the trapping of the
  superfluid side at $K=2/\pi$.
\item \lean{QuasiPeriodicBound}: increments of a finite trigonometric sum are bounded by $2\sum|a_k|$, so a growing
  mean-square displacement excludes a quasi-periodic orbit.
\item \lean{VortexWinding}, \lean{TopologicalProtection}: the plaquette winding rule and the integer conservation of
  winding, from earlier rounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Code}\label{app:code}

The field solver, the instrument and the estimators are Python (NumPy) in the repository
(\texttt{exploration/pgpe/}: \texttt{pgpe.py}, \texttt{vortex\_transport.py}, \texttt{transport\_estimators.py},
\texttt{dielectric\_modes.py}); a Rust port of the instrument is in rusty-SUNDIALS (\texttt{qf-pgpe}, example
\texttt{vortex\_transport.rs}), cross-checked against the Python one to $5\times10^{-7}$ in positions. The two pieces a
reader needs to reproduce the measurements are short.

\noindent\textbf{The corrected imprint} (Appendix~\ref{app:imprint}; \texttt{theta1} is the Jacobi function with
nome $e^{-\pi}$, \texttt{s} the solver with grid \texttt{s.N}, box \texttt{s.L}, projector \texttt{s.P}):
\begin{lstlisting}
def imprint_v2(s, c, pos, q):
    x = np.arange(s.N) * s.dx; X, Y = np.meshgrid(x, x, indexing="ij"); Z = X + 1j * Y
    def factor(Zs):                                   # theta-function phase of all vortices
        ph = np.ones(Zs.shape, complex)
        for (xj, yj), qj in zip(pos, q):
            t = theta1(np.pi * (Zs - (xj + 1j * yj)) / s.L); u = t / (np.abs(t) + 1e-300)
            ph = ph * (u if qj > 0 else np.conj(u))
        return ph
    ph = factor(Z)
    jx = np.angle(np.mean(factor(Z + s.L) * np.conj(ph)))        # phase jump across x = L
    jy = np.angle(np.mean(factor(Z + 1j * s.L) * np.conj(ph)))   # ... and across y = L
    ph = ph * np.exp(-1j * (jx * X + jy * Y) / s.L)             # uniform gradient: periodic for any dipole moment
    vx = np.angle(np.roll(ph, -1, 0) * np.conj(np.roll(ph, 1, 0))) / (2 * s.dx)   # velocity of the imprinted phase
    vy = np.angle(np.roll(ph, -1, 1) * np.conj(np.roll(ph, 1, 1))) / (2 * s.dx)
    amp = np.sqrt(1.0 / (1.0 + 0.5 * (vx**2 + vy**2)))        # Bernoulli density: 0 at the cores, 1 - v^2/2 far away
    c2 = s.modes(s.psi(c) * ph * amp)
    return c2 * np.sqrt(s.norm(c) / s.norm(c2))
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent\textbf{The estimators} (\texttt{pv\_velocity} returns the torus point-vortex velocity at every vortex,
\texttt{pv\_energy} the Weiss--McWilliams energy; \texttt{U} are the unwrapped tracked positions, shape (time,
vortex, 2)):
\begin{lstlisting}
V = np.array([pv_velocity(np.mod(U[k], L), q, L) for k in range(nt)])      # v_s,i(t)
H = np.array([pv_energy(np.mod(U[k], L), q, L) for k in range(nt)])        # point-vortex energy
S = (V**2).sum(axis=(1, 2))                                               # sum_i |v_s,i|^2
I = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(0.5 * (S[1:] + S[:-1]) * np.diff(t))])
alpha_energy = -np.polyfit(2 * I, H, 1)[0]                                # dH/dt = -2 alpha sum |v_s|^2
Bv = -q[None, :, None] * np.stack([-V[..., 1], V[..., 0]], -1)             # -q z x v_s
CA = np.concatenate([np.zeros_like(V[:1]), np.cumsum(0.5*(V[1:]+V[:-1])*dt, 0)])
CB = np.concatenate([np.zeros_like(V[:1]), np.cumsum(0.5*(Bv[1:]+Bv[:-1])*dt, 0)])
D, A, B = U[lag:] - U[:-lag], CA[lag:] - CA[:-lag], CB[lag:] - CB[:-lag]
M = [[(A*A).sum(), (A*B).sum()], [(A*B).sum(), (B*B).sum()]]
one_minus_alpha_prime, alpha_regression = np.linalg.solve(M, [(A*D).sum(), (B*D).sum()])
e = D - one_minus_alpha_prime * A - alpha_regression * B                 # residuals -> <|e|^2>(lag) = c0 + 4 eta lag
\end{lstlisting}
