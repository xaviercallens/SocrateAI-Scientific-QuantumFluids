\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A quantized vortex in a thermal two-dimensional Bose gas is dragged by the normal fluid (friction $\alpha$),
pushed sideways by it (transverse coefficient $\alpha'$) and kicked by it (diffusion $\eta$). The only
first-principles values of $\alpha$ for a bosonic classical field stop at $0.18\,T_{\rm BKT}$ and carry no error
bar on $\alpha'$~\cite{Shukla2014}; the fluctuation--dissipation relation tying $\eta$ to $\alpha$ was proposed for
test~\cite{Mehdi2023} and found violated by two orders of magnitude in one experiment~\cite{Neely2024}. We
measure the three coefficients in the projected Gross--Pitaevskii equation --- microcanonical, no damping term, no
noise put in by hand --- with sub-grid vortex tracking and estimators that are identities of the dissipative
point-vortex model, machine-checked in Lean~4. Every hypothesis, gate and decision rule was committed before the
corresponding run; the instrument failed its $T=0$ control twice before it passed, an earlier gate verdict built
on the flawed instrument is withdrawn, and both are reported. Results: at $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14,0.27,0.43$,
$\alpha=0.0062(4),\,0.014(2),\,0.02(1)$ --- size- and estimator-independent, $\alpha\approx0.24\,\rho_n/\rho$;
$\alpha'$ is zero within $1\%$ once the $T=0$ compressible pair speed is subtracted, excluding the Iordanskii value
$\rho_n/\rho$ at $18\sigma$ (the registered Iordanskii-like prediction is refuted); the Einstein relation is
inconclusive by the registered rule, with the one admitted temperature at $\eta K/\alpha=2.1\pm0.7$ and the others
at $1.4$--$3$ --- not $100$. Single pairs stop shrinking while the phonon band takes the impulse they shed
(a self-generated counterflow, with a published precedent~\cite{KrstulovicBrachet2011}); the decisive box-size
control is not run. Separately: the single non-equilibrium pair that corrupts the stiffness estimator in finite
boxes decays by $4$--$27\times$ given $L^{z}$-scaled time, one of six $L=192$ trajectories still unfinished at
$t=14\,500$; the pre-registered mode-by-mode dielectric relation \emph{fails} in the thermal pair gas (coherence
$0.06$--$0.28$) while the vortex-charge structure factor equals the Kosterlitz--Thouless polarisability
$\Sigma d^2$ from the measured pair sizes to $5$--$21\%$; and the Josephson offset $\eta_{g_1}K-1=+0.3$ survives at
$L=192$ in equilibrium, independent of window and torus geometry. No physics is claimed as new; the numbers, the
refutations and the method are.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is measured and why it is open}

With the normal fluid at rest a vortex of charge $q$ moves at
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf v=(1-\alpha')\,\mathbf v_s-\alpha\,q\,\hat z\times\mathbf v_s ,
  \label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
$\mathbf v_s$ the superfluid velocity induced at its position by the other vortices~\cite{Iordanskii1964,Sonin1997}.
For $\alpha\to0$ the two sides of a long controversy predict different $\alpha'$: no transverse force from the
normal fluid, $\alpha'=0$~\cite{ThoulessAoNiu1996}, or the Iordanskii force, $\alpha'=\rho_n/\rho$~\cite{Iordanskii1964,Sonin1997}.
If the field is the vortex's heat bath, friction and diffusion obey $\eta=\alpha T/(2\pi\rho)$
($\hbar=m=k_B=1$)~\cite{Mehdi2023}. For a bosonic classical field, $\alpha(T)$ exists only below
$0.18\,T_{\rm BKT}$~\cite{Shukla2014}, $\alpha'$ changes sign within its noise there and ``deviates'' in 3D Kelvin-wave
fits~\cite{KrstulovicBrachet2023}; the Einstein relation has never been tested in a closed field; helium-film
analyses assume it~\cite{AHNS1980}; the one cold-atom measurement of $\eta$~\cite{Neely2024} is $100\times$ the
prediction. A review of the field is~\cite{Sergeev2023}.

A second question is static. The superfluid stiffness is read from the transverse current response,
$\rho_n(k)=\langle|J_T(k)|^2\rangle/(TL^2)$; in the Coulomb-gas picture vortices reduce it through their
polarisation~\cite{VallatBeck1994,Faulkner2017,KimMinnhagenOlsson1999}. For a compressible Bose field no functional
form at finite $k$ exists: classical-field work extrapolates to $k\to0$ by fits of convenience and has reported
negative superfluid fractions attributed to noise~\cite{Foster2010,GawrylukBrewczyk2019}.

\paragraph{Model and assets.} Projected Gross--Pitaevskii equation ($\hbar=m=g=1$, $dx=\xi/2$, cutoff at half the
grid wavenumber), energy conserved to $10^{-7}$--$10^{-6}$ over every run; boxes $L=64$ (transport) and
$L=192$ (equilibration, dielectric relation); a calibrated two-window thermometer; $T_{\rm BKT}(L{=}64)=0.821$
from the round-2 ladder; thermal states with $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14,0.27,0.43$ and no thermal vortex (raw count
$\le0.36$), and one at $0.56$ with thermal pairs (report only). Pre-registrations, amendments and gate records
are files in the repository (\texttt{docs/designs/PGPE\_FRICTION\_PREREG.md} and amendments A1, A1.1, A1.2;
\texttt{PGPE\_ALPHAPRIME\_PREREG.md}; \texttt{PGPE\_EINSTEIN\_PREREG.md}; \texttt{PGPE\_DIELECTRIC\_PREREG.md}
with amendments D-A1, D-A2), each committed before the data it governs existed.

\section{The instrument, and what it took to trust it}

\paragraph{Estimators (identities of the model).} Writing the configuration's point-vortex Hamiltonian $H$
(Weiss--McWilliams on the torus~\cite{WeissMcWilliams1991}), Eq.~\eqref{eq:motion} is a skew part plus
$-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\nabla H$, so $\dot H=-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla H|^2$ for any configuration and any $\alpha'$:
$\alpha=-\Delta H/(2\!\int\!\sum_i|\mathbf v_{s,i}|^2dt)$ is the \emph{energy estimator}, pairing-independent and
blind to $\alpha'$ (\lean{energy\_dissipation}). The displacement regressed on the two orthogonal predictors
$\int\mathbf v_s\,dt$ and $-q\!\int\hat z\times\mathbf v_s\,dt$ gives $1-\alpha'$ and $\alpha$ separately
(\lean{centre\_velocity\_identity}); its residuals' mean square against lag gives $\eta$, quoted only if the
scaling exponent lies in $[0.8,1.2]$. For a stochastic pair with drift $-2\alpha\mathbf d/|\mathbf d|^2$ and
diffusion $D=2\eta$, the Kosterlitz--Thouless pair distribution $|\mathbf d|^{-K}$ carries zero probability current
iff $DK=2\alpha$ (\lean{zero\_flux\_iff}); the Einstein statistic is $R_E=\eta K/\alpha$.

\paragraph{Gates.} Synthetic Langevin trajectories with known $(\alpha,\alpha',\eta)$ are recovered to
$7\%$, $0.002$ and $25\%$ (G0; its first attempt failed on a generator defect, on file). The $T=0$ control (G1)
failed on its first run and exposed two defects of the imprint used by this programme since round~2: the
theta-function phase is periodic only when $\sum q\mathbf r\in L\mathbb Z^2$ --- a single pair carries a phase
step $2\pi d/L$ along a whole line --- and the amplitude factor $[r^2/(r^2+2)]^{1/2}$ per vortex has a $1/r^2$
tail four times the Bernoulli depletion, whose sound drifts pair separations by $0.6$--$0.8$ in $400$ time units
at $T=0$: an apparent $|\alpha|$ of $0.007$--$0.010$, as large as the thermal value at $0.14\,T_{\rm BKT}$
(cf.\ \cite{Rorai2013} on product-ansatz dipoles). With a periodic phase and a Bernoulli amplitude
(\texttt{imprint\_v2}) G1 passes: $|\hat\alpha|=4\times10^{-4}$, $1-\hat\alpha'=0.9986$, no diffusion, single-pair
field momentum constant to $3\times10^{-10}$ and $0.96$--$0.99$ of $2\pi nd$, separation constant at $9.82$ ---
a pair at $T=0$ neither shrinks nor diffuses~\cite{LucasSurowka2014}. The thermal known answer (G2,
$T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$, two antiparallel pairs so that no net impulse is shed) gives $\alpha=0.00705\pm0.00012$
inside the window set from~\cite{Shukla2014} before the run.

\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Withdrawn.} An earlier known-answer gate of this study (single dipoles, the old imprint, a coarse-grained
detector biased low by $\approx1$ in separation) was reported as ``PASS, narrowly'' with an unexpected
dependence of $\alpha$ on pair size and a stalled pair. All three were artefacts of that instrument and are
withdrawn (LEDGER CLAIM-078). The stall motivated a hypothesis --- the impulse shed by a shrinking pair in a
closed box drives a phonon wind that stalls it (\lean{wind\_stall}) --- which stays registered; its status is in
Section~\ref{sec:wind}.
\end{failbox}

The three registered criteria pass: no intrinsic size dependence (ratios in $[0.7,1.43]$), monotone in $T$, the two

\paragraph{Transverse coefficient.} Registered estimator: weighted regression of $\hat\alpha'$ on $\rho_n/\rho$ over
the three temperatures and $T=0$. Values $+0.0014$, $-0.0100\pm0.0015$, $-0.0154\pm0.0029$, $-0.0093\pm0.0106$;
slope $b=-0.31\pm0.05$. By the rule, ``no transverse force'' (the Iordanskii window $b\in[0.6,1.4]$ is excluded at
$18\sigma$); \textbf{the registered prediction --- Iordanskii-like --- is refuted.} The raw $\alpha'$ is negative at
$7\sigma$ and $5\sigma$, which neither theory predicts; two post-hoc controls (estimator unchanged in the verdict)
account for it. In a periodic box a single pair carries net momentum, so the fluid has a mean velocity $u=P/nL^2$
absent from the point-vortex prediction; in the fluid frame a $T=0$ pair moves at the torus point-vortex speed to
$+0.4\%$ for $d\ge8$, $+1\%$ at $d=6$ and $+3$--$4\%$ at $d=3$--$4$ (the Jones--Roberts compressible correction;
production runs have $|u|\le2\times10^{-3}$ and two counter-moving pairs, so the frame correction to them is
$\le0.0002$). Subtracting that $T=0$ baseline at the matched pair sizes leaves $\alpha'=-0.006$, $-0.011$, $-0.006$
with a mapping uncertainty of the same order: \textbf{$\alpha'=0$ within about $1\%$}, and the Iordanskii values
$+2.7\%$, $+5.3\%$, $+9.4\%$ are excluded. The round-3 configuration (eight imprinted vortices, $d=16$) gives
$1-\alpha'=1.0012$, consistent.

\paragraph{Diffusion and the Einstein relation.} $\eta$ from the residuals of the two-coefficient regression
against lag, quoted only where the scaling exponent lies in $[0.8,1.2]$ (rule E2):
$3.7\times10^{-4}$ (exponent $0.71$, not quoted), $(1.08\pm0.35)\times10^{-3}$ ($0.82$, quoted),
$(1.8\pm0.4)\times10^{-3}$ ($1.28$, not quoted). With $K=2\pi n_s/T$, $R_E=\eta K/\alpha=3.2,\,\mathbf{2.1\pm0.7},\,1.4$.
\textbf{Verdict by the registered rule: inconclusive} (one admitted temperature; two required). Informally and
against the rule, the vortex diffuses at $1.4$--$3$ times the Einstein value, decreasing with temperature --- not
the $100\times$ of~\cite{Neely2024} and not clearly $1$. The exponents are the reason for caution, and were examined
after the fact (amendment E-A1, filed after the analysis it concerns and labelled a retrodiction): over lags
$20$--$400$ the exponent rises with $T$ ($0.71\to0.82\to1.28$), but over lags $\ge100$ the two cooler temperatures
give $1.1$--$1.2$; the sub-diffusion at $0.14\,T_{\rm BKT}$ is a short-lag regime, not a bounded orbit. The
machine-checked statement behind that reading is one-directional: a finite quasi-periodic sum has a bounded
mean-square increment, so growth refutes a regular island and a plateau never proves one
(\lean{QuasiPeriodicBound.lean}).

\section{The phonon wind of a closed box}\label{sec:wind}

A pair of separation $d$ carries impulse $2\pi\rho_sd$; in a periodic box its decrease has nowhere to go but the
phonons, which then drift at $u=2\pi\rho_s(d_0-d)/(\rho_nL^2)$ and reduce the friction drive,
$\dot d=-2\alpha(1/d-u)$, stalling at $b=[d_0+(d_0^2-2\rho_nL^2/\pi\rho_s)^{1/2}]/2$ when the root is real
(\lean{wind\_stall}). At $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14$, $L=64$, $d_0=12$ should stall ($b=10.2$ in the plane formula,
$\approx9$ torus-corrected) and $d_0=8$ should not. Two single-pair runs of $4000$ time units: neither pair
annihilates; one shrinks $11.6\to9.3$ and stays there (late $d^2$ slope $4\%$ of the early one: criterion W1
met), the other shrinks $11.2\to10.2$ and re-expands to $11.2$ (W1 failed by the letter, the pair moving back toward
the stall point); the momentum of the modes with $|k|>1$ follows the impulse the pair sheds with slopes $0.68$ and
$0.52$ (W3, window $[0.5,1.2]$, met), falling back when the pair re-expands. Zero-impulse pairs at the same
temperature shrink steadily from $10$ to $7$--$8$. The decisive control --- the same pair in an $L=96$ box, where
no stall is predicted --- has not been run; the hypothesis is \textbf{unresolved with first support}. Its object
has a precedent: in the truncated GPE a thermal cloud with net momentum relative to the condensate is a metastable
state decaying by Arrhenius ring nucleation~\cite{KrstulovicBrachet2011}; ours would be the same counterflow,
self-generated by a shrinking pair. On the torus the zero-circulation dipole is a relative equilibrium of the
point-vortex flow~\cite{ModinViviani2020}, so the wobble of the stalled pair is bath-driven by theorem; its spectrum
over $t\in[2000,4000]$ puts only $32$--$36\%$ of the variance in three lines, led by the slowest resolvable
frequency: a stalled equilibrium perturbed by a bath, not a quasi-periodic orbit. If the mechanism holds,
closed-box friction measurements on single pairs at low temperature are contaminated unless
$\rho_nL^2\gg\pi\rho_sd_0^2/2$ --- a condition the literature's truncated-GPE measurements should be checked against.

\section{Box-scale vortex charge, equilibration and the stiffness estimator}

Seven of eighteen large-box runs of an earlier round reached $n_s/n<0$ with the same vortex number as healthy
partners. A pre-registered test on their vortex positions refuted the Onsager-cluster reading (no like-sign
clustering, positive vortex temperature, $98$--$99.7\%$ of vortices paired with an opposite neighbour); post hoc,
the point-vortex transverse current $n_{\rm eff}^2(2\pi)^2|\rho_q(k)|^2/k^2$ computed from positions alone tracks the
measured one across all eighteen runs with $r=0.996$: the anomaly is one $O(1)$ unscreened pair at the box scale ---
the finite-$k$ form of the polarisation term of~\cite{VallatBeck1994,Faulkner2017}, the single free vortex of
early superconducting films, and the post-quench surviving pair that Chu and Williams asked simulations to look
for~\cite{ChuWilliams2001}. Such a pair has Boltzmann weight $(L/\xi)^{-K}\lesssim10^{-9}$: it is a remnant of the
initial relaxation. Equilibrium finite-size stiffness is monotone in $L$~\cite{ProkofevSvistunov2002,Hasenbusch2005};
coarsening under conservative dynamics gives $z\approx1.7$--$1.8$~\cite{GroszekBillam2026} ($L^2\ln L$ for
dissipative XY~\cite{Bray2000,JelicCugliandolo2011}; $L^2/\ln L$ Hamiltonian XY~\cite{Nam2012}), so the warm-up that
cured $L=128$ should scale to $\approx11\,000$--$14\,600$ at $L=192$. Registered before the runs: continuing the six
$L=192$ trajectories to $t=14\,500$ should admit at least five of six (E1), bring the vortex transverse current below
$0.45$ in every admitted run (E2), and drop it by at least $2\times$ in the two anomalous ones (E3):

at this temperature exceeds $13\,500$ for some trajectories --- above the $L^2$ and $z\approx1.7$ estimates, as
the $L^2\ln L$ form would have it; one trajectory is one trajectory. The offsets $\eta_{g_1}K-1$ on the
equilibrated window are $+0.20,+0.16,+0.30$ ($e=1.00,1.10,1.20$): positive at every size and energy once

\paragraph{Josephson relation.} On these equilibrated $L=192$ states the coherence exponent fitted with the exact
torus Green function on $r\in[8,32]$ gives $\eta_{g_1}K-1=+0.31\pm0.11$, unchanged by the fit window ($16$--$48$)
and equal to the naive-window value: the $12$--$27\%$ excess reported at $L\le64$ is neither a geometry factor
(cf.\ ``close to unity''~\cite{HolzmannBaym2007}) nor a finite-size transient. It persists in equilibrium at
$L=192$. What remains is the estimator pair itself --- $\eta$ from $g_1$ against $K$ from current correlators on a
fixed-momentum microcanonical field, for which no fluctuation formula has been derived --- or the renormalisation
of the bare stiffness by amplitude fluctuations. Reported, not explained; the earlier statement that this excess is
``unexplained finite-size'' is amended.

\section{The finite-wavevector dielectric relation}

Pre-registered claims: mode by mode and snapshot by snapshot, $J_T(k)=n_{\rm eff}\,(2\pi i\rho_q(k)/|k|)$ plus a
phonon part uncorrelated with $\rho_q$ (D1: coherence $\gamma^2\ge0.4$ on every shell $|m|^2\le16$ in $\ge5$ of 6
runs), with one real $k$-independent $n_{\rm eff}$ (D2); and (D4) the plateau of $\langle|\rho_q(k)|^2\rangle/k^2$
equal to $\Sigma_{\rm pairs}d^2/2$ from the minimum-cost matching of the detected vortices, within $30\%$. Five gate
runs on imprinted pair gases failed on the amplitude's known answer and are on file; the coherence criterion
passed in all five ($\gamma^2=1.000$ at $T=0$ with the corrected imprint): the relation exists for an imprinted pair
gas, with an amplitude that rises with pair size ($0.922,0.971,0.991$ at $d=5,8,14$ --- a tight pair carries less
current than two point vortices). On the six equilibrated $L=192$ states ($170$--$830$ vortices, almost all in
tight pairs, $T/T_{\rm BKT}\approx0.6$--$0.85$):
\textbf{D1 fails (1 of 6), D2 fails (1 of 6), D4 passes ($0.79$--$0.95$ in all five runs without a box-scale pair).}
The coherence between the transverse current and the detected charge density at the same $k$ and $t$ is
$0.06$--$0.28$; the one run that passes is the one still carrying a box-scale pair. So the $r=0.996$ of the
diagnosis above and the $0.987$ of the triage probe were, as the pre-registration itself warned, correlations of
run-averaged powers carried by box-scale pairs: the diagnostic detects one unscreened pair and does not extend to a
dielectric function of the thermal pair gas. What survives is D4: the bound-pair gas has a $k$-independent
charge structure factor out to $k\xi\approx0.13$ whose value is the Kosterlitz--Thouless polarisability
$\varepsilon-1\propto\Sigma d^2$ computed from the measured pair sizes, $5$--$21\%$ lower (screening of dipoles by
dipoles), read directly from vortex positions in a Bose field~\cite{KosterlitzThouless1973,Kosterlitz1974,FanWilliams2022}.
How much of the stiffness that polarisability accounts for is what D1's failure leaves open; the first analysis
code carried a $k^2$ unit error in D4, corrected to the registered formula before anything else was changed.

\section{What this establishes, and what it does not}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Established (pre-registered criteria, or controls stated as such).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item At $T=0$ a vortex pair neither shrinks nor diffuses over $400$ time units and moves at the torus point-vortex
  velocity to $0.14\%$ in the fluid frame; tight pairs ($d\le6$) move $1$--$4\%$ faster (compressible correction).
\item $\alpha(T)=0.0062(4),\,0.014(2),\,0.02(1)$ at $T/T_{\rm BKT}=0.14,0.27,0.43$, size- and estimator-independent;
  $\alpha\approx0.24\,\rho_n/\rho$.
\item $\alpha'$ is not Iordanskii-like ($18\sigma$); after the $T=0$ baseline it is zero within $\sim1\%$.
\item The depressed-stiffness states of finite boxes carry one unscreened box-scale pair, not clusters; given
  $L^z$-scaled time it decays by $4$--$27\times$ (six of six admitted; one trajectory not finished at $14\,500$).
\item The vortex-charge structure factor of the thermal pair gas is $k$-independent and equals the
  Kosterlitz--Thouless polarisability from the measured pair sizes to $5$--$21\%$.
\item The Josephson offset $\eta_{g_1}K-1=+0.31\pm0.11$ survives at $L=192$ in equilibrium, independent of window
  and torus geometry.
\item Theorems (standard axioms): energy dissipation of any skew-plus-gradient vortex flow, the $d^2$ law and
  lifetime bound, the centre-velocity identity, the wind stall, the zero-flux/Einstein equivalence, the
  pair-polarisation form and response ceiling, the charge-density/matching bound, the Kosterlitz-flow invariant,
  and the bounded increments of quasi-periodic signals.
\end{itemize}
\end{keybox}
\begin{failbox}
\textbf{Refuted, inconclusive, or not established.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Refuted:} the registered Iordanskii-like prediction for $\alpha'$; the mode-by-mode dielectric relation
  in the thermal pair gas; the Onsager-cluster reading of the depressed-stiffness states; the earlier
  ``saturation'' reading of the finite-size offset.
\item \emph{Inconclusive by rule:} the Einstein relation (one admitted temperature; $1.4$--$3\times$ informally).
\item \emph{Unresolved:} the phonon-wind mechanism (first support; $L=96$ control missing); the cause of the
  Josephson offset; the current carried by tight thermal pairs; why $\alpha\propto\rho_n$ with coefficient $0.24$.
\item \emph{Scope:} a Rayleigh--Jeans field with one cutoff, $mg=1$, two dimensions; nothing about quantum gases or
  helium follows without a cutoff study.
\end{itemize}
\end{failbox}

\paragraph{Method, in one paragraph.} The point-vortex model is a variational projection of the field onto the
manifold of vortex positions; the coefficients measured here are the statistics of the field's leak out of that
manifold --- the same object that a recent many-body experiment reads from the other end, by projecting a quantum
state back onto a variational manifold at every step and asking how much coherence survives~\cite{Dong2026}. The
hypotheses tested here were themselves ranked by an automated five-minute-probe loop under priors fixed before
any probe ran, and the literature behind the design is a local database of $774$ entries ($382$ arXiv identifiers
verified against the arXiv API), described in the repository.

\paragraph{Process record.} Three gate failures on the transport instrument (one generator, two imprint), one
withdrawn gate verdict, one tracking amendment after a production pass with no data; five dielectric gate failures
on the amplitude's known answer with the coherence criterion untouched; one registered prediction refuted, one
inconclusive by rule, one amendment filed after the analysis it concerned and labelled a retrodiction; one unit
error in analysis code caught and corrected before use. Data, code, pre-registrations, the ledger of claims and the
Lean sources are in the repository.
