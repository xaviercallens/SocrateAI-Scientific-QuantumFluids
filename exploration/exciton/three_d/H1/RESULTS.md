## 1. The bound used (statement and proof sketch)

Fourier convention: f̂(y) = ∫_{R^d} f(x) e^{−2πi x·y} dx. Potential g(x) = e^{−α|x|²}.

**Proposition (Cohn–Kumar two-point bound, periodic version).** Let f: R^d → R be a radial Schwartz function with
f(x) ≤ g(x) for all x ≠ 0 and f̂(y) ≥ 0 for all y. Then every periodic configuration P of unit density has Gaussian energy
per particle E_g(P) ≥ f̂(0) − f(0).

*Proof sketch.* Write P = ∪_{j=1}^N (Λ + v_j) with Λ a lattice of covolume |Λ| = N (unit density), v_j distinct mod Λ.
The energy per particle is

  E_g(P) = (1/N) Σ_{j,k=1}^N Σ'_{ℓ∈Λ} g(ℓ + v_k − v_j),

where Σ' omits the N terms with ℓ + v_k − v_j = 0 (j = k, ℓ = 0). Since f ≤ g off the origin, the same sum with f is
not larger; adding back the omitted terms, each equal to f(0), gives

  E_g(P) ≥ (1/N) Σ_{j,k} Σ_{ℓ∈Λ} f(ℓ + v_k − v_j) − f(0).

Poisson summation in this convention reads Σ_{ℓ∈Λ} f(ℓ + v) = (1/|Λ|) Σ_{y∈Λ*} f̂(y) e^{2πi y·v}, Λ* = {y : y·ℓ ∈ Z for
all ℓ ∈ Λ} (the left side is Λ-periodic in v; its Fourier coefficient at y ∈ Λ* is (1/|Λ|)∫_{R^d/Λ} Σ_ℓ f(ℓ+v) e^{−2πi y·v} dv
= (1/|Λ|) ∫_{R^d} f(x) e^{−2πi y·x} dx = f̂(y)/|Λ|; the sign of the exponent in the Poisson identity follows from the sign
in f̂). Summing over j, k:

  (1/N) Σ_{j,k} Σ_ℓ f(ℓ + v_k − v_j) = (1/(N|Λ|)) Σ_{y∈Λ*} f̂(y) |Σ_k e^{2πi y·v_k}|² ≥ (1/(N|Λ|)) f̂(0) N² = f̂(0),

dropping the y ≠ 0 terms (all ≥ 0 because f̂ ≥ 0) and using N/|Λ| = 1. Hence E_g(P) ≥ f̂(0) − f(0). ∎

Sign check on the trivial certificate f = g (admissible: ĝ(y) = (π/α)^{d/2} e^{−π²|y|²/α} > 0): E ≥ (π/α)^{d/2} − 1,
i.e. in d = 1, α = 1: E_Z(1) = 0.7726372 ≥ √π − 1 = 0.7724539 (true); α = π: E ≥ 0 (true). The direction and the
normalisation are therefore consistent with the direct sums below.

## 2. Ansatz, closed form, LP

f̂(y) = e^{−π|y|²/β} p(|y|²), p a polynomial of degree ≤ K = 30 (as registered). The polynomial is written in the
radial Hermite–Laguerre eigenbasis of the Fourier transform in R^d,

  φ_k(y) = e^{−π|y|²} L_k^{(d/2−1)}(2π|y|²),  φ̂_k = (−1)^k φ_k,

so that f̂(y) = Σ_k c_k φ_k(y/√β) gives, in closed form, f(x) = β^{d/2} Σ_k (−1)^k c_k φ_k(√β x). This is the same
function space as "Gaussian times a polynomial of degree ≤ 30 in |y|²"; the basis is only better conditioned than
monomials. The eigenfunction identity was checked numerically (Hankel-transform quadrature, d = 1, 2, 3, k ≤ 9,
agreement 1e-16) and the scaled pair (f, f̂) was checked by quadrature at three radii (agreement 1e-15); these
quadratures are checks only — no numerical Fourier transform enters the LP. The scaled Laguerre functions
e^{−t/2} L_k^{(a)}(t) are generated by the three-term recurrence.

LP (scipy.optimize.linprog, method `highs`, feasibility tolerances 1e-10; cvxpy is not installed):

  maximise f̂(0) − f(0) = Σ_k c_k L_k^{(a)}(0) (1 − (−1)^k β^{d/2})  subject to
  f(r_i) ≤ e^{−α r_i²} on the registered grid r_i = 6i/2000, i = 1..2000, plus a tail grid of 600 points on (6, 24];
  f̂(y_j) ≥ 0 on y_j = 12 j/12000, j = 0..12000;
  top-coefficient sign (tail behaviour): if πβ > α, the top coefficient of p is forced > 0 (margin 1e-10 on c_30);
  if πβ ≤ α, f must be ≤ 0 at infinity (it decays slower than g), which forces c_30 = 0 and c_29 < 0 (odd effective
  degree; then p > 0 and f < 0 at infinity).

Cutting planes: after each solve, f − g and −f̂ are evaluated on 6×10^5 points (uniform plus random), every local
maximum of the violation is polished by bounded 1-D maximisation (xatol 1e-13), and the polished points plus the
400 worst grid points are appended; 2 rounds in the coarse β scan, 8 rounds for the final β candidates.

β scan: β ∈ (α/π) × {0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 1, 1.2, 1.5, 2, 3} at coarse settings (extended by one step when the best is at an
edge), then the best β and the geometric means with its two neighbours at full settings; the best admissible bound is
kept. Admissible = tail certificates pass and the polished residual violations are ≤ 5e-9.

Tail certificates (40-digit arithmetic, `GaussLP.tail_certificates`): beyond the grids the exponentials underflow in
double precision, so the monomial coefficients of the polynomial factors of f and f̂ are formed in mpmath, all real roots
are bounded by the Cauchy bound S_big, the sign at infinity is read off the top coefficient, and on [24², S_big]
(resp. [12², S_big]) the envelope e^{−decay·s} Σ_m |coef_m| s^m is maximised on a dense log grid; the certificate passes
if the sign at infinity is right and the envelope is ≤ 1e-12 (then any excursion of f above g, or of f̂ below 0, beyond
the grids is below 1e-12).

Registered verification: the final f is re-checked for f ≤ g on 10^5 random radii (80 % uniform on (0, 24], 20 %
log-uniform on [1e-6, 24]); f̂ ≥ 0 is checked on 10^5 random |y| in [0, 24] as well.

**Numerical validity.** Pointwise constraints cannot enforce exact non-negativity of a function with (near-)double zeros:
the LP always exploits the gap between neighbouring constraint points, producing dips of depth ~ curvature × spacing²/4.
The polished residual violations (reported below, ~1e-9 or less) are therefore the honest numerical tolerance of each
bound; a strictly valid certificate can never exceed the true minimum energy, so the sign of (LP − E_Z) in d = 1 is a
direct measurement of this discretisation error.

## 3. Reference energies (direct lattice sums)

Unit density. FCC and BCC are the body-centred tetragonal cell of `../AB/bain_selfdual.py` at c/a = √2 and 1
(Σ_{v≠0} e^{−α|v|²} over all lattice vectors with |v| ≤ R, R = max(7, (60/α)^{1/2}), so that the tail is below 1e-26).
Cross-check: E_FCC(π) = E_BCC(π) = 0.231536266184, the value quoted in the pre-registration from `bain_selfdual.py`.
E_Z(α) = 2 Σ_{n≥1} e^{−α n²}. A₂ (triangular) at unit density has basis a(1,0), a(1/2, √3/2), a = (2/√3)^{1/2}.

**H0 FAILED → H1 was stopped as registered.** The d = 3 bounds for α ∈ {2, π, 4, 8} and the d = 2 extra were **not

Reading of the failure (not a re-interpretation of the gate). At α = 1 the "bound" lies 2.0e-9 *above* E_Z, which a strictly
valid certificate cannot do: this is the discretisation error of pointwise constraints described in §2, here of order 1e-9.
At α = π all three full-setting LPs produce bound values within 3e-10 of E_Z, but their polished constraint violations
(2e-8 to 5e-8) exceed the 5e-9 admissibility tolerance fixed in the code before the run; the coarse scan's solutions
(violations ~1e-7, accepted only under the looser coarse tolerance) lie 4e-8 *above* E_Z(π), again an invalid-by-value
discretisation artefact, and were never admissible at the full tolerance. So the code reproduces E_Z(π) in value but cannot
certify it at the required precision with K = 30 and the registered grids. Since the H1 gate thresholds (1e-6, 1e-3) are
far above this 1e-8 level, a d = 3 run would probably have been informative; it was nevertheless not run, because the
registered rule ties it to H0. A re-registration (e.g. exact rational certificates or a sum-of-squares formulation for the
non-negativity constraints, so that admissibility is proved rather than sampled) is the natural next step and is left
open.

## 5. Deviations (dated 2026-10-12)

1. The admissibility tolerance (polished violation ≤ 5e-9) and the coarse tolerance were fixed in `run_h1.py` before the
   run but were not written in the pre-registration; they are what made H0 fail at α = π.
2. The registered verification "10^5 random points for f ≤ potential" was extended to f̂ ≥ 0 as well.
3. Tail grids (600 points on (6, 24], f̂ grid to |y| = 12) and the 40-digit tail certificates are additions to the
   registered grid; they only make admissibility harder.
4. The run itself completed (it stopped on the H0 rule at 08:53); the session that launched it was cut by a context
   compaction before writing this section, which was written afterwards from `run.log` and `summary.json` only; no code
   was changed and nothing was rerun.
