decisiveness × leverage × cost / 125, so that 1.0 is the best possible.

## Reading of the table

- **D1 is the largest contribution if the control supports it, and it is decided tonight.** If the L = 96 pair
  shrinks through the L = 64 stall separation and keeps shrinking, the stall is the box's, the mechanism is
  supported by three of three registered tests, and the follow-up is a clean quantitative study of a new object:
  a counterflow generated by a single vortex pair, with a parameter-free stall law and a direct observable
  (`n_k − n_{−k}`). The implication — single-pair friction measurements in closed boxes are biased unless
  `ρ_nL² ≫ πρ_sd₀²/2` — applies to published truncated-GPE work. If the L = 96 pair also stalls near the same
  separation, the mechanism is refuted as written, and D1 becomes a one-paragraph report.
- **D2 is the best value per unit of effort and is independent of tonight's outcome.** It is analysis of data we
  have, it is decisive by construction (three estimators, one relation), and it fixes a number that enters every
  Josephson-relation statement of this programme and of the classical-field literature.
- **D7 continues the main established result** and asks the question a reader will ask first: is 0.24 a property
  of the model or of the cutoff? It needs one new cutoff (a day of bases) and reuses everything else.
- D3 is the deepest physics question raised by the dielectric failure, but its decisive measurement (the current
  form factor of pairs smaller than the healing length) is not available with the present instrument; it is the
  right second step after D7 and D2, not the next one.
- D4–D6 are worth doing and cheap-ish, but each answers a narrower question than D1, D2 or D7.

## Selection

**Primary: D1, conditional on W2 tonight.** If W2 supports the mechanism (the registered criterion: no stall at
L = 96, late `d²` slope at least 50 % of the early one or annihilation), the next pre-registered campaign is the
counterflow study: stall separation against `d₀`, `L` and `T` (plane and torus laws, `wind_stall` extended to the
torus drive), the normal-fluid drift read from the occupation anisotropy, and the relaxation of the drifting phonon
gas (does it nucleate vortices, as imposed counterflow does?). **If W2 refutes it, the primary becomes D7** (the
friction law against cutoff and temperature, with the classical phonon-scattering prediction), which is the
continuation of the result the review called the paper's cleanest.

**In parallel, now: D2.** It is analysis only, it does not touch the machine, and its outcome goes into the same
paper's Josephson paragraph (currently "reported, not explained"). Pre-registration to be filed before the first
estimator is computed: the three candidate estimators, the prediction that exactly one of them moves the product
to `1 ± 0.1`, and what is concluded if none does.

Not selected now, with reason: D3 (instrument-limited), D4 (closes a verdict rather than opening a question),
D5 (competes with a group that has GPUs), D6 (a useful rule, but a rule).
